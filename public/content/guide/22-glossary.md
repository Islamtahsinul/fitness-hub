# Glossary

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - About 180 terms used across this guide, in alphabetical order, each explained in a sentence or two.
> - The guide is written for experienced lifters, so the everyday lifting terms are kept short. They're here so the hub's wording is unambiguous, not to teach you to lift.
> - Where a term has a number that depends on you (your Zone 2, your protein target, your weight band), the formula is given, worked through for the **example profile** (a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult) where it helps. Your own numbers are on **Nutrition** and **Settings**.
> - Gates appear by the names the hub shows: **Now**, **Good arm OK**, **Light use / rehab started**, **Grip cleared** and **Full clearance**. They always outrank everything else.
> - If you meet a term that isn't here, note it in your weekly review and ask.

## Terms A–Z

### 0–9

- **1RM (one-rep max).** The heaviest weight you can lift once with good form. You never test it in this programme; the hub estimates it instead (see *e1RM*). Your pre-injury bests are history for now (see *ego lifting*).
- **90/90 breathing.** Lying on your back with hips and knees bent at 90°, feet on a wall or bench, taking slow breaths with long exhales. It's the `breathing-90-90` drill in your mobility routine.

### A

- **A&E.** Accident and Emergency, the hospital emergency department. For a life-threatening emergency, call 999.
- **ACSM.** The American College of Sports Medicine, an exercise-science body whose position stands this guide cites (for example on fluids).
- **Adaptation.** Your body's response to training stress, made during recovery rather than in the session.
- **Add-ons.** Extra sessions the hub adds on set days while one gate is met and a higher one isn't yet. The good-arm add-on runs from Good arm OK in Phases 0 and 1; the physio rehab add-on runs from Light use / rehab started until Full clearance, and appears once your physio's exercises are entered on **Your arm**. Add-ons never count towards your weekly strength sessions or adherence.
- **Adherence.** The share of planned sessions you actually do, recorded as a percentage in your weekly review. RED days and fasting easy days are neutral: they don't count against it.
- **Aerobic and anaerobic.** Aerobic energy is made with oxygen and powers long, steady efforts like hiking. Anaerobic energy is made quickly without enough oxygen, for hard efforts you can only keep up briefly.
- **Aerobic threshold.** Roughly the top of easy, all-day effort. The drift test in chapter 10 estimates yours; lab tests measure related thresholds (lactate or ventilatory).
- **Anti-rotation, anti-extension, anti-lateral flexion.** Core exercises where the job is to *resist* movement: twisting (Pallof press), arching (dead bug) or bending sideways (suitcase carry).
- **Ascent.** The total height you climb on a route, in metres. On a hike it matters as much as the distance.
- **Association.** When two things tend to occur together in a study. It doesn't prove that one causes the other (see *observational study*).

### B

- **Baseline.** Your first set of test results and measurements. Every later test is compared with it. Most are taken on days 1–3; the leg-press baseline is your best leg-press calibration set of Re-entry Week, and your goal is about +30% on it by the end of Phase 1 (chapter 01). Your resting-HR baseline comes from 2–4 weeks of morning pulses (chapter 17).
- **BDA.** The British Dietetic Association, the UK professional body for dietitians. Its food fact sheets are cited in the nutrition chapters.
- **Bearing.** A direction measured with a compass in degrees from north. It lets you walk a straight line in poor visibility.
- **Bioimpedance (BIA).** How smart scales estimate body fat: a tiny electrical current through your feet. Fine for weight, unreliable for body-fat % in an individual, so ignore a smart scale's body-fat figure.
- **Bird dog.** On all fours, extending the opposite arm and leg while keeping your trunk still. It bears weight through the wrist, so it needs Full clearance.
- **BMI (body mass index).** Weight in kg divided by height in metres squared: 24.7 for the example profile (80 kg, 180 cm). It can't tell muscle from fat, so it's only a rough check.
- **BMR (basal metabolic rate).** The energy your body uses at complete rest. For the example profile, by the Mifflin–St Jeor equation: 1,780 kcal a day for a man, or 1,614 kcal for a woman.
- **BOAST and BSSH.** The British Orthopaedic Association Standards for Trauma, and the British Society for Surgery of the Hand. Chapter 18 cites their guidance.
- **Bracing.** Tightening the muscles all the way around your trunk to keep your spine stable under load. Before clearance, brace moderately and breathe out on the effort. No maximal breath-holding.

### C

- **Calibration set.** In Re-entry Week, the last set of an exercise, taken to about RIR 2 after two easier sets, to find your working load. Your leg-press calibration set is the baseline for your strength goal (chapter 01).
- **Callus (bone).** The new bone tissue that bridges a fracture as it heals. Rigid plates can let bone heal with little visible callus, so if you have plates, ask how your clinician reads your X-ray.
- **Carrying rule.** The one rule for carrying anything, whose home is chapter 18. Training carries wait for Grip cleared and then follow the grip ladder. Until Light use / rehab started: nothing in your injured hand or on that shoulder; a light bag (3 kg or less) on your uninjured shoulder or a waist pack is fine, and on walks a waist pack only (2 kg or less). From Light use / rehab started: a light day pack on both shoulders, starting at 2–4 kg.
- **Casein.** The main, slow-digesting protein in milk. Milk, quark, yoghurt and cottage cheese before bed supply it.
- **Centenarian Decathlon.** Peter Attia's idea: list the physical tasks you want to be able to do late in life, then plan backwards ("backcasting") to what you train now. Your "functional decathlon" in chapter 01 uses the same idea.
- **Certification (halal).** A check by an independent body that a food or product meets its halal standard. UK bodies differ, for example on whether stunning before slaughter is accepted (chapter 15).
- **Circuit.** Several exercises done back-to-back with little rest, repeated for rounds or for a set time.
- **CoFID.** McCance and Widdowson's Composition of Foods Integrated Dataset, the UK government's food-composition tables. Most food values in the hub come from it.
- **Cold water immersion.** Ice baths and cold plunges. Done straight after lifting, they blunt muscle gains, and in this programme they wait until full clearance.
- **Concentric.** The lifting phase of a rep. The opposite of *eccentric*.
- **Contour line.** A brown line on a map joining points of equal height. On OS Explorer maps the gap between lines is usually 5 m (10 m in some mountain areas). Lines close together mean steep ground.
- **Copenhagen plank.** A side plank with the top leg on a bench, which loads the inner thigh. It needs weight through an arm, so it waits for Full clearance.
- **Creatine and creatinine.** Creatine monohydrate is the supplement (3–5 g a day): optional, and chapter 16 decides when to start. Creatinine is a breakdown product measured in blood tests of kidney function; creatine can raise it, so tell your doctor you take it.
- **Cross-education.** Training one limb improves strength in the opposite, untrained limb. It's why training your uninjured arm, once Good arm OK is set, may help your injured side.
- **CRPS (complex regional pain syndrome).** An uncommon condition that can follow a fracture: persistent pain out of proportion to the injury, often burning, sometimes with swelling and colour or sweating changes. Contact your clinic or GP within days (chapter 18).
- **Cyclic sighing.** A breathing drill: breathe in through the nose, top up with a short second sniff, then breathe out slowly and fully. Five minutes a day improved mood in a Stanford trial.

### D

- **Dead bug.** Lying on your back, arms up, lowering the opposite leg (and later arm) slowly while keeping your lower back still. The programme's version is legs only.
- **Dead start.** How the programme uses a plate-loaded leg press: staff set the bottom safety stops once at your bottom position and leave the top catches released, so the sled rests on the stops between reps and you never have to work a catch. Chapter 04 explains it.
- **Deload.** A lighter week that lets your body absorb the training: half the sets of the same exercises at the same loads, with easy intervals. Planned deloads are written into your programme, roughly every 5–6 weeks, and the hub halves the sets in those weeks. It never adds or moves one by itself: it suggests one, and the rule is to take one early if you're AMBER on 3 or more days in a week or your main lifts drop two sessions running (chapter 17).
- **Detraining.** Losing fitness, strength and muscle when you stop training. After an injury layoff you're detrained, and the injured arm most of all. See *muscle memory*.
- **DEXA (DXA).** A low-dose X-ray scan that measures fat, lean mass and bone. It's the yardstick smart scales are tested against.
- **DOMS (delayed-onset muscle soreness).** Muscle soreness that peaks a day or two after unfamiliar or hard training. After a layoff, familiar lifts bring it on too, and it fades within a couple of weeks of regular training. Soreness of 7/10 or more makes it an AMBER day.
- **Double progression.** Build reps across the range on every set, then add load and drop back to the bottom. The hub's load steps: leg press 10 kg, machines and cables 2.5 kg, dumbbells 2 kg.
- **Drift test.** A 40–60-minute steady treadmill walk where you compare the heart rate of the second half with the first to estimate your aerobic threshold (chapter 10).
- **Dynamometer.** A hand-held device that measures grip strength in kilograms.

### E

- **e1RM (estimated one-rep max).** A 1RM calculated from a set of reps. The hub uses the Epley formula, kg × (1 + reps ÷ 30), for progress charts and your leg-press goal only.
- **Easy-day mode.** What the hub applies on an AMBER day, and automatically on fasting days: half the sets, Zone 2 only, no intervals, and the same loads ("Hold: same load, RIR 3–4"). You can also switch it on yourself when you log a session.
- **E numbers.** Codes for permitted food additives. Some can be animal-derived: E120 (cochineal or carmine) comes from insects, and E471 can come from animal or plant fat (chapter 15).
- **Eccentric.** The lowering phase of a rep. Your programme often slows it to 3 seconds.
- **ECG (electrocardiogram).** A recording of the heart's electrical activity, the gold standard that heart-rate monitors are tested against.
- **EFSA.** The European Food Safety Authority, whose caffeine safety limits (up to 400 mg a day) this guide uses.
- **Ego lifting.** Lifting to match a number in your head, such as your pre-injury bests or the person next to you, rather than the plan. The comeback version is adding sets or load because you "feel fine". You know how to train; the arm sets the pace (chapter 20).
- **Electrolytes.** Salts such as sodium lost in sweat. Salty snacks replace them on long or hot hikes.
- **Evidence tags.** **[Strong evidence]** means consistent findings from good trials or reviews. **[Moderate evidence]** means some supporting trials or observational studies, with real limits. **[Expert opinion]** means coaching experience or consensus without strong trials.
- **External fixator.** A frame outside the limb, attached to the bone with pins, that holds a fracture still. It's sometimes used for severe open fractures until internal fixation is safe.

### F

- **Fall risk.** How likely an exercise is to cause a fall. Each exercise in the hub is rated low, medium or high. Until full clearance, a fall is your biggest threat.
- **Farmer carry.** Walking with a heavy weight in each hand: the heart of "farmer strength". It starts light to moderate at Grip cleared, by the grip ladder. The standards are in chapter 09's table.
- **Fasting mode.** The hub's setting for any fast (for example Ramadan, another religious fast or planned fasting): add the dates under **Settings → Fasting periods**. On those dates every day is AMBER, so easy-day mode applies automatically, and the hub shows a fasting reminder: train after you break your fast if you can, stop at any dizziness or light-headedness, and no new lifts this period. Your phase dates don't move, and the hub gives no weight verdict during the fast or for the 14 days after it (chapter 14).
- **Fortnightly verdict (weight).** Every two weeks the hub judges your weight trend against your band and gives one verdict: below, in or above. It needs at least 10 weigh-ins in the fortnight, and skips fasting periods and the 14 days after them. The goals card and the coach notes always show the same verdict (chapter 14).
- **Fracture clinic.** The hospital outpatient clinic that checks how your fracture is healing. It decides your clearances. Record your next appointment on **Your arm**.
- **Fracture-related infection.** An infection of the bone or metalwork after a fracture. Signs such as wound discharge, fever, redness and heat are red flags (chapter 18).
- **FSA.** The Food Standards Agency, whose food-safety rules (leftovers within two days, rice within 24 hours) the batch-cooking plan follows.

### G

- **Gates.** The five clearance levels that control what you can train: Now (no clearance needed), Good arm OK, Light use / rehab started, Grip cleared and Full clearance. They're cumulative: clearing a higher gate counts for every gate below it.
- **Gelatine.** A protein made from animal skin and bones, usually pork or beef. It's in jelly sweets, some yoghurts and desserts, and most capsules, so check labels if you avoid it (for example if you eat halal or vegetarian).
- **Glycogen.** The carbohydrate stored in your muscles and liver. Topping it up adds water weight, which is why your weight jumps a little in the first weeks.
- **Good-arm training.** Training your uninjured arm on its own, seated, three days a week. Only allowed once Good arm OK, or a higher gate, is set.
- **GP.** Your general practitioner (family doctor).
- **Grid reference.** A map position on the OS National Grid. Read eastings (along) before northings (up). Four figures pin down a 1 km square, and six figures narrow it to about 100 m.
- **Grip ladder.** The hub's rule of thumb for returning to carries, with your injured hand's grip as a percentage of your uninjured hand's: at least 70% for light holds and carries (with Grip cleared and your physio's OK), at least 80% for moderate farmer carries, and at least 90% for heavy carries and hanging (with Full clearance).
- **Grip strength.** How hard you can squeeze, in kg. It's a marker of overall strength that is linked with long-term health.
- **GTG (grease the groove).** Pavel's method of frequent, easy practice sets spread through the day or week, always far from failure, to build strength as a skill.

### H

- **Halal.** Permissible under Islamic dietary law. In this guide's optional halal notes: halal meat, no pork, gelatine only if certified, and your own choices on rennet, stunning, shellfish and trace alcohol.
- **Hike ladder.** The ten levels on the hub's Hikes page, from a 30-minute flat walk (Level 1) to a multi-day trail with a full pack (Level 10). Each level has a gate.
- **Hinge.** The hip-hinge pattern behind deadlifts, RDLs, bridges and hip thrusts.
- **Hollow hold.** Lying on your back, lower back pressed down, with shoulders and legs lifted. The programme's version keeps the knees tucked.
- **HRmax (maximum heart rate).** The highest heart rate you can reach: about 187 bpm for a 30-year-old (the example profile), by the Tanaka formula (208 − 0.7 × age). You can override it with a measured value.
- **HRV (heart-rate variability).** The small variations in time between heartbeats. Higher than your own normal often signals good recovery. It's noisy, and numbers from different devices aren't comparable, so watch trends rather than single days.
- **Hypertrophy.** Muscle growth.

### I

- **Iftar and suhoor.** In Ramadan, the meal that breaks the fast at sunset, and the meal before dawn. If you fast, train after iftar if you can (chapter 14).
- **Implementation intention.** A specific plan: "I will [do X] at [time] in [place]", or "If [situation], then [action]". It makes you more likely to follow through.
- **Incline walk.** Walking uphill on a treadmill: a key hiking-engine exercise in your programme.
- **Informed Sport.** A batch-testing scheme that checks supplements for banned substances. Useful if you're ever drug-tested; optional otherwise.
- **Intervals.** Hard work alternated with easy recovery, for example 30 seconds hard and 90 seconds easy.
- **ISSN.** The International Society of Sports Nutrition, whose position stands on protein, creatine and caffeine this guide cites.
- **Isometric.** Holding a position under tension without moving, as in a wall sit.

### K

- **Kettlebell.** A cast-iron ball with a handle, and the main tool of Pavel's *Simple & Sinister*. Not every gym has them, so the hub always lists an alternative.
- **Kinesiophobia.** Fear of movement, driven by fear of pain or re-injury. It's common after injury, and physios work on it routinely.

### L

- **Lean mass (fat-free mass).** Everything in your body that isn't fat: muscle, bone, organs and water. Gains in lean mass are mostly what "building muscle" means.
- **Lean-gain band.** **+0.15 to +0.3 kg a week** on your 7-day average: the hub's weight band once you're at or above your usual pre-injury weight (chapter 14).
- **Leg press.** A machine where you push a weighted sled away with your legs. On a plate-loaded 45° model with safety catches, the programme uses a dead start.
- **Leucine.** An amino acid that helps switch on muscle building. Meat, fish, dairy, eggs and soya are rich in it.

### M

- **MAF heart rate.** Phil Maffetone's "maximum aerobic function" heart rate: 180 minus your age, then an adjustment. For the 30-year-old example profile, 180 − 30 = 150; while recovering from injury, Maffetone suggests −10 → 140 (his −10 covers recovery from an operation and rehab). Treat it as a ceiling for easy days, not a target.
- **Malunion.** When a broken bone heals in the wrong position.
- **Meta-analysis.** A study that pools the results of many earlier studies to give an overall answer.
- **Mifflin–St Jeor equation.** The formula the hub uses for BMR: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age, then +5 for men or −161 for women.
- **Minimum standards.** Your non-negotiables: the few things you do even in your worst week.
- **Minimum viable session (MVS).** The shortest session that still counts: about 25–30 minutes of easy bike, the mobility routine and some Zone 2. In the hub it's **Short on time?** on Today and Log a session.
- **Missions.** The big hiking targets in chapter 13, each tied to the earliest phase and gate it could happen in.
- **Mobility.** Your ability to move a joint actively through its range, with control.
- **MSK.** Musculoskeletal: to do with bones, joints and muscles, as in an "MSK physio".
- **Muscle memory.** Muscle and strength you've built before tend to come back faster on retraining than they were first built. Retraining studies support it, though not all of them, and the mechanism in humans is still debated (chapter 01). It's why your weight band is higher while you regain to your pre-injury weight.
- **MWIS.** The Mountain Weather Information Service, which writes forecasts for upland areas of Britain.

### N

- **Neck check.** A rule of thumb for training when ill: symptoms only above the neck allow an easy Zone 2 walk at most; anything below the neck or all over means rest. After a fracture, a fever is also an arm red flag.
- **Never miss twice.** You can miss a planned session, but never two in a row. On the second day, do at least the minimum session. RED days and fasting easy days are neutral, so they don't count as misses.
- **Non-union (nonunion).** When a fracture fails to heal. Risk factors include smoking, infection and open fractures.
- **Nordic curl.** A kneeling exercise that lowers your body forwards under hamstring control. The programme's assisted version waits for Full clearance.
- **NSAIDs.** Non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, such as ibuprofen and naproxen. Ask your fracture clinic before using them for training soreness.

### O

- **Observational study.** A study that watches what happens without assigning a treatment. It can show an association, but not cause and effect.
- **Open (compound) fracture.** A broken bone with a wound or break in the skin nearby. The infection risk is higher, which is why fever and wound discharge are red flags.
- **ORIF (open reduction and internal fixation).** Surgery in which broken bones are realigned through an incision and held with plates, screws or rods. Plates are internal fixation; people often call the hardware "metalwork".
- **OS Explorer and Landranger maps.** Ordnance Survey's walking maps. Explorer maps are 1:25,000 and show the most detail. Landranger maps cover a wider area at 1:50,000 (not verified).
- **Outcome, performance and process goals.** What you want to achieve, what you want to be able to do, and what you do each week. Process goals are the ones you fully control.
- **Overreaching and overtraining.** Overreaching is a short-term dip in performance after hard training that recovers with rest. Overtraining is a longer-lasting, harder-to-fix state. The readiness rule and deloads guard against both.

### P

- **Pallof press.** A cable exercise where you press a handle straight out from your chest and resist being twisted. Anti-rotation core work, from Grip cleared.
- **PAR-Q+.** The Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire for Everyone: a seven-question health screen to complete before exercising. It's valid for 12 months, or until your health changes.
- **Patrick step.** Standing on one leg on a low step, you bend the standing knee forward until the heel of your other, straight leg touches the floor in front. It trains knee control for descents.
- **Phase.** A block of weeks with one main aim. A phase starts only once its entry gate is met; until then the hub repeats the later weeks of your current phase.
- **Physio (physiotherapist).** Your rehab specialist. Anything your physio prescribes overrides this programme. If you haven't been referred yet, ask at your next fracture-clinic appointment.
- **Plyometrics.** Jumping, hopping and bounding drills. Off-limits until Full clearance because of the impact and fall risk.
- **Pre-injury weight.** Your usual weight before the injury, entered in Settings ("Your usual weight before the injury", optional). While your 7-day average is below it, the hub uses the regain band; without it, a middle band of +0.2 to +0.4 kg a week.
- **Progression and regression.** A harder or an easier version of an exercise. Every exercise in the hub lists both.
- **Progressive overload.** Gradually asking more of your body (more reps, load, sets or time) so it keeps adapting.
- **Pronation and supination.** Rotating your forearm palm-down (pronation) or palm-up (supination). Both forearm bones take part, which is why your physio may measure both in degrees.
- **Protein density.** Grams of protein per 100 kcal, shown in the hub's food table. Ten or more means a lean protein food.
- **Protein target.** Your daily protein goal: 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight. The hub's default is 1.8 g/kg, which is about 144 g a day for the 80 kg example profile.

### R

- **Radius and ulna.** The two forearm bones: the radius on the thumb side and the ulna on the little-finger side. A forearm fracture can involve one or both.
- **Ramadan.** The Islamic month of fasting from dawn to sunset. Its dates move about 11 days earlier each year, so check them nearer the time. If you fast, add the dates under **Settings → Fasting periods** (chapter 14).
- **RCT (randomised controlled trial).** A study that randomly assigns people to a treatment or a comparison. The strongest single study design for cause and effect.
- **RDL (Romanian deadlift).** The bodyweight single-leg version is in your plan now; loaded versions wait for their gates.
- **Re-entry Week.** Phase 0 of your programme: one week to set up each machine one-handed and find your working loads, with 2 easy sets (RIR 3–4) and one calibration set (RIR 2).
- **Readiness rule.** The morning check that sets your day (chapter 17 has the details). A weight on its own doesn't count as a check-in.
  - **RED:** any arm red flag ticked, or arm pain 7/10 or more. No training today, including your physio exercises, until you've been advised; contact your fracture clinic today, or NHS 111 / 999 as the red-flag guidance says.
  - **AMBER:** resting heart rate at least 5 bpm above your 7-day average (once there are 5 readings since your last change of method), sleep under 6 hours, arm pain 4/10 or more, arm pain 2 or more points above its 7-day average, soreness 7/10 or more, energy 2/5 or less, or a fasting day. Easy day: halve the sets, Zone 2 only, no intervals, hold loads.
  - **GREEN:** none of the above. Train as planned.
- **Red flags (arm).** Warning signs that need medical advice: pain getting worse, new swelling, redness or heat, discharge from the wound, fever or feeling unwell, numbness or tingling, a cold or discoloured hand, deformity or a "giving" feeling, and any fall onto the arm.
- **Regain band.** **+0.25 to +0.5 kg a week** on your 7-day average, used while you're below your pre-injury weight, because lost muscle comes back faster than new muscle is built (chapter 14).
- **Rehab.** Exercises for your injured arm, prescribed by your physio. They're added to your week from Light use / rehab started, once you've entered your physio's exercises on **Your arm**; until then it says "Waiting for your physio's programme".
- **Rennet.** The enzyme used to set most cheese. Animal rennet comes from calves; "suitable for vegetarians" cheese uses a non-animal rennet. Whether it matters is your choice.
- **Resting heart rate (RHR).** Your heart rate at rest, measured as a 60-second pulse on waking. A rise of 5 bpm or more above your 7-day average makes it an AMBER day. If you change how you measure it (for example, from a wearable's overnight number to the manual pulse), press "I changed how I measure resting HR" in Settings, and the hub re-baselines over the next 5 readings.
- **Rhabdomyolysis.** A rare but serious breakdown of muscle after very hard training the muscles aren't ready for; after a layoff, that can include lifts you know well, so the risk is highest in your first weeks back. Dark (cola-coloured) urine with severe muscle pain or swelling after training needs same-day medical advice (chapter 17).
- **RIR (reps in reserve).** How many more good reps you could have done when you stopped. RIR 2 means two left in the tank. Estimates get more accurate the closer you are to failure, which is one job of the Re-entry Week calibration set.
- **rMSSD.** A standard measure of HRV, used by many apps and devices.
- **ROM (range of motion).** How far a joint moves, in degrees. Your physio may track your wrist and forearm ROM.
- **RPE (rating of perceived exertion).** How hard something felt, on a 1–10 scale. For a lifting set, RPE 8 is roughly RIR 2. The hub also asks for a whole-session RPE.
- **Rucking.** Walking with a loaded rucksack. Light rucking opens at Light use / rehab started, and heavier loads wait for Full clearance.

### S

- **S&S (Simple & Sinister).** Pavel's kettlebell programme: 100 one-arm swings and 10 get-ups. The men's "Simple" standard is 32 kg. A long-term goal after Full clearance.
- **Safety catches (stops).** Adjustable stops on a machine or rack that hold the weight if you can't. On a plate-loaded leg press, the bottom stops are set for the dead start (chapter 04).
- **SAID principle.** Specific Adaptation to Imposed Demands: your body adapts to exactly what you train.
- **Scree.** Loose, broken rock on mountain slopes that shifts underfoot. High fall risk, so Full clearance only.
- **SFG.** A StrongFirst-certified kettlebell instructor.
- **Sinus (wound).** A small channel from deep tissue to the skin that may leak fluid. After a fracture it's treated as a sign of infection: contact your clinic (chapter 18).
- **Sit-to-stand test (30-second).** Count how many times you can stand up fully from a chair and sit back down in 30 seconds, arms folded. A simple test of leg strength.
- **Sled.** A weighted sled you push or drag. Not every gym has a sled belt, so the programme has substitutes (**Settings → My gym**), and pushing waits for its gate.
- **SMART goal.** A goal that is Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Time-bound. A useful checklist, not a magic formula.
- **Smith machine.** A barbell that runs on fixed vertical rails. It needs both hands, so it waits for its gate.
- **Soya mince or chunks (TVP).** Dried, textured soya protein, about half protein by weight, and the cheapest protein per 20 g in chapter 15.
- **Stability.** Controlling your position and resisting unwanted movement. Peter Attia calls it the foundation of all training.
- **Subject access request.** Your legal right to ask any organisation for a copy of the personal data it holds about you, such as your history from a wearable's app.
- **Suitcase carry.** Walking with a weight in one hand while staying tall: as much a core exercise as a grip one. Your programme uses no carries until Grip cleared.
- **Surplus (calorie surplus).** Eating more energy than you use. The default surplus is about 300 kcal a day (for the male example profile, a target of about 3,060 kcal). It supports muscle gain and bone healing. No dieting while the bone heals.
- **Systematic review.** A review that searches for and appraises all the studies on a question using set methods, often with a meta-analysis.

### T

- **Talk test.** A Zone 2 check: you can speak in full sentences but would rather not.
- **Tanaka formula.** HRmax = 208 − 0.7 × age, derived from a meta-analysis of 351 studies (18,712 people) and confirmed in a lab study.
- **TDEE (total daily energy expenditure).** All the energy you use in a day: BMR × an activity factor. For the example profile at the hub's default factor of 1.55, about 2,760 kcal for a man or 2,500 kcal for a woman.
- **Technical failure.** The point where you can't do another rep with good form. Stop before it.
- **Tempo notation.** Four numbers for the seconds in each phase of a rep: lowering, pause at the bottom, lifting, pause at the top. `3-1-1-0` means 3 seconds down, a 1-second pause, 1 second up, and no pause at the top.
- **Tension.** Deliberately tightening muscles to create strength and stability: a core skill in Pavel's teaching.
- **Tibialis raise.** Leaning your back on a wall and lifting your toes towards your shins, which strengthens the front of the lower leg for downhill walking.

### U

- **UKAD.** UK Anti-Doping, which warns that no supplement is guaranteed free of banned substances.
- **Union.** A healed fracture: the bone has bridged and is strong enough for normal use. Only your fracture clinic can confirm it.

### V

- **Valsalva manoeuvre.** Holding your breath and straining against a closed throat, which spikes blood pressure. No maximal Valsalva before clearance. Brace moderately and breathe out on the effort.
- **VO2max.** The maximum rate at which your body can use oxygen: the standard measure of aerobic fitness, and strongly linked with a longer life.

### W

- **Waist-to-height ratio.** Your waist divided by your height. The NHS advises keeping it under 0.5, which means a waist under 90 cm for the 180 cm example profile.
- **Wall press (rehab).** A physio-graded push against a wall. It's the only weight-bearing exercise for the injured wrist allowed before Full clearance (from Grip cleared).
- **Warm-up and ramp-up sets.** Easy movement before training, then lighter practice sets before your first working set. Your strength warm-up is 5 minutes on the bike, the mobility routine, then 2 ramp-up sets.
- **Weight-bearing (through the wrist).** Putting body weight through your hand and wrist, as in push-ups, planks, all-fours positions and side planks on the forearm. Full clearance only.
- **Whey.** The fast-digesting milk protein sold as protein powder. It comes from cheese-making, so if you eat halal, look for halal certification.

### Z

- **Zercher.** Holding a barbell or sandbag in the crooks of your elbows for squats or carries. A long-term option, well after full clearance.
- **Zone 2.** 60–70% of HRmax: about **112–131 bpm** for the 30-year-old example profile. You can speak in full sentences but would rather not, and you can breathe through your nose. The backbone of your hiking engine.
- **Zones (Z1–Z5).** Heart-rate bands as a percentage of HRmax: Z1 50–60, Z2 60–70, Z3 70–80, Z4 80–90, Z5 90–100. For the 30-year-old example profile (HRmax 187): Z1 94–112, Z2 112–131, Z3 131–150, Z4 150–168 and Z5 168–187 bpm.

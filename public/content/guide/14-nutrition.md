# Nutrition — Fuel, Muscle & Bone Healing

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - **Your numbers come from formulas:** Mifflin–St Jeor BMR × your activity factor (default 1.55), plus a 300 kcal surplus, and **1.8 g of protein per kg**. For the example profile (a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult) that's about **3,060 kcal** (male) or **2,800 kcal** (female) and **144 g** of protein a day. The **Nutrition** page works out yours.
> - **No dieting while your bone heals.** Gain at your band's rate on your scale's 7-day average: **+0.25–0.5 kg a week** while you're below your usual pre-injury weight, then **+0.15–0.3**, or **+0.2–0.4** until you add that weight in Settings. The hub gives one verdict a fortnight. This chapter owns the bands.
> - Eat **about 0.4 g/kg of protein** (roughly 30 g) at each of 4–5 meals, **budget-first**: milk, eggs, yoghurt, lentils, soya mince, tinned fish and chicken do the heavy lifting. [Chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md) has the meals, the prices, and halal-friendly and vegetarian swaps.
> - Bone needs **calcium (700 mg)**, **vitamin D (10 µg, October to March)** and enough protein and energy. Don't smoke, and skip the megadoses: they don't speed healing.
> - **Long walks:** start eating at about 60 minutes, the guide's one fuelling rule. **Fasting periods** (for example Ramadan): add the dates under **Settings → Fasting periods**, train after you break your fast if you can, and hold your weight steady. There's no nudge to gain while you fast, or for 14 days after.

## Why food is your most powerful tool right now

You have three jobs on the go: **healing** a fracture (and, if you had surgery, the wound and the tissue around any plates), **regaining the muscle** you've lost during the layoff and then building towards **+5 kg**, and **fuelling** 45–90 minutes of training six mornings a week. Your arm has to wait for its gates; eating well doesn't. It's **fully available now** and helps all three jobs at once.

This chapter is the *what* and *why*: [chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md) turns it into meals and shopping, [chapter 16](16-supplements.md) covers supplements, and [chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md) covers sleep and recovery.

## Working out your numbers

The hub calculates these from your profile and your latest weigh-in, so they creep up as you gain. The examples use the **example profile: a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult**. Put your own figures into the formulas, or read them off the **Nutrition** page.

| What | How it's worked out | Example: male | Example: female |
|---|---|---|---|
| BMR (resting burn) | Mifflin–St Jeor: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age, then +5 for men or −161 for women | 800 + 1,125 − 150 + 5 = **1,780 kcal** | 800 + 1,125 − 150 − 161 = **1,614 kcal** |
| Maintenance (TDEE) | BMR × 1.55, the hub's default activity factor | 2,759: about **2,760 kcal** | 2,502: about **2,500 kcal** |
| Daily target | Maintenance + 300 kcal surplus (the hub rounds to the nearest 10) | about **3,060 kcal** | about **2,800 kcal** |
| Weight band | **+0.25–0.5 kg a week** below your pre-injury weight, **+0.15–0.3** at or above it, **+0.2–0.4** if it isn't set | Judged once a fortnight (below) | The same |
| Protein | 1.8 g/kg; the full range, 1.6–2.2 g/kg | **144 g** (range 128–176 g) | The same |
| Protein per meal | 0.4 g/kg | about **32 g** | The same |
| Carbohydrate | What's left after protein and fat, at 4 kcal per gram | about **355–470 g** | about **310–415 g** |
| Fat | 20–35% of your calories, at 9 kcal per gram | about **68–119 g** | about **62–109 g** |
| Fibre | NHS adult advice | **30 g** | The same |
| Calcium | UK adult reference | **700 mg** | The same |
| Vitamin D | October to March (NHS). See [chapter 16](16-supplements.md) | **10 µg (400 IU)** | The same |

**If your activity setting changes, so does your target.** At the hub's lowest setting (1.375) the male example's target is about 2,750 kcal, and at the highest (1.725) about 3,370 kcal; for the female example it's about 2,520 and 3,080. Chapter 15 has meal templates at about 2,700, 3,000 and 3,300 kcal, and the hub marks the one closest to your target. If your target sits well below 2,700, chapter 15 shows how to trim a template without dropping protein.

> [!TIP]
> These equations are estimates. Two people with the same height, weight and age can differ by a few hundred calories a day. Treat your target as a well-informed first guess, and let your weight trend correct it.

## How fast to gain: your weight band

This is the guide's one weekly rate; other chapters point here. The hub picks your band from one optional number in **Settings**, "Your usual weight before the injury", and judges every band on your scale's 7-day average.

| Your 7-day average | Your band | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Below your pre-injury weight | **+0.25 to +0.5 kg a week** (the regain band) | You're rebuilding muscle you've had before, and lost muscle comes back faster than new muscle is built |
| At or above your pre-injury weight | **+0.15 to +0.3 kg a week** (the lean-gain band) | New muscle beyond your previous best comes slowly after years of training, so gaining faster mostly adds fat |
| No pre-injury weight set | **+0.2 to +0.4 kg a week** | A middle band until you add the number |

**The evidence behind the bands.** Retraining studies find that muscle and strength you've had before come back faster than they were first built, though not every study agrees and the mechanism in humans is still debated ([chapter 01](01-assessment-and-goals.md) has the studies). **[Moderate evidence]** A review of off-season bodybuilding nutrition suggests gaining about 0.25–0.5% of body weight a week for novice and intermediate lifters, and says advanced lifters should be more conservative. **[Moderate evidence]** For the 80 kg example that's 0.2–0.4 kg a week, so the lean-gain band sits at its cautious end. The exact band edges are **[Expert opinion]**.

**What it means in practice.** Take the 80 kg example. If they weighed about 84 kg before the injury, the regain band gets them back there in roughly 8–16 weeks, and then the lean-gain band takes over. With no pre-injury weight set, their 7-day average should rise by about 2.5–5 kg over the first 12 programme weeks: the 12-week goal in [chapter 01](01-assessment-and-goals.md). How much of the gain is muscle, and the regain-then-new-muscle timeline, are in [chapter 08](08-building-muscle.md).

**How to weigh:**

1. Step on the scale **every morning**, after the toilet and before food or drink, on the same spot of hard floor.
2. Log the weight in the hub's check-in. Daily swings of 0.5–1 kg are just water and food, so **the 7-day average is the number that matters.**
3. If it's a smart scale, ignore its body-fat, muscle and "metabolic age" readings. [Chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md) explains why.

### The fortnightly verdict

The hub judges your weight one fortnight at a time, and the fortnights never overlap. Each needs at least **10 weigh-ins**; with fewer, that fortnight gets no verdict, so weigh every morning. You get **one verdict per fortnight: below, in or above your band**, and the goals card and the coach notes always show the same one. To check it yourself, compare your 7-day average with the one 2 weeks earlier, and halve the difference to get your change per week.

| Verdict | What to do |
|---|---|
| Below your band, and your weight is falling | Add **250–300 kcal a day**, and check you're eating every planned meal |
| Below your band (flat, or rising too slowly) | Add **150–200 kcal a day**, such as a glass of milk and a banana. If it happens twice in a row, move up a step: the next template up in chapter 15, or another 150–200 kcal |
| In your band | Change nothing |
| Above your band | Take off **100–150 kcal a day**, such as the pre-gym dates or a slice of toast, staying above maintenance. If it happens twice in a row, move down a template step, but never below the maintenance figure on your Nutrition page |

These step sizes are **[Expert opinion]**. During a fasting period, and for the 14 days after it, there's no verdict and no nudge to gain (see "Fasting periods" below).

> [!NOTE]
> Expect a jump of 0.5–1 kg in the first week or two as your muscles store more glycogen and water now that they're training again, especially if you start creatine. If your first verdict says "above your band" because of it, don't cut food: wait for the next one.

## Why you must not diet while the bone heals

Your programme has a firm rule: **no dieting while the bone heals. A small surplus is the default.**

- **Healing is building work.** New bone forms on a collagen (protein) scaffold while the wound and soft tissues repair too. A review of fracture healing concludes that "a healthy diet with adequate amounts of both macro- and micronutrients is essential". **[Expert opinion]**
- **Poor nutrition goes with worse outcomes.** In a 2022 systematic review, poor nutritional status predicted more complications after fracture surgery. **[Moderate evidence]** Most patients were older and often malnourished, so the lesson for you is simply: don't create a shortfall.
- **Restricting food harms bone.** The British Dietetic Association (BDA) lists being underweight and restricted diets as risks for weak bones. **[Moderate evidence]**
- **Muscle regrows best in a surplus.** You've lost muscle during the layoff, the injured arm most of all, and you won't rebuild it, or add more, while eating less than you burn. **[Expert opinion]**

## Protein: the building blocks for muscle and bone

### How much

| Source | Daily protein |
|---|---|
| Morton and colleagues' meta-analysis of protein supplementation trials | Muscle gains levelled off at about **1.6 g/kg** (128 g for the 80 kg example). The upper confidence limit was about **2.2 g/kg** (176 g) |
| International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) | **1.4–2.0 g/kg** for most people who exercise |
| BDA | **1.6–1.7 g/kg** for muscle gain with resistance training |
| **The hub** | **1.8 g/kg** by default: **144 g** for the example |

There is **[Strong evidence]** that 1.6 g/kg or more, with resistance training, maximises muscle gain for most people. The meal templates give 131–152 g (1.6–1.9 g/kg for the 80 kg example), a sensible margin while you're healing. **[Expert opinion]** If you're lighter, the same templates give you more per kilogram; if you're heavier, add from chapter 15's add-ons.

### How to spread it

- **About 0.4 g/kg a meal (roughly 30 g) over 4–5 meals.** Schoenfeld and Aragon suggest about 0.4 g/kg per meal across at least four meals. **[Moderate evidence]** The ISSN suggests 20–40 g every 3–4 hours. **[Strong evidence]**
- **A big meal isn't wasted.** A 2023 study found that 100 g of protein after training gave a bigger and longer (over 12 hours) muscle-building response than 25 g. It's one study in young men, so **[Moderate evidence]**. Spread your protein, but don't worry if lunch is your biggest meal.
- **Have some slow protein before bed.** The ISSN says 30–40 g of casein (the main milk protein) before sleep raises overnight muscle protein synthesis. **[Moderate evidence]** The templates' 300 ml of pre-bed milk gives about 11 g. To reach 30 g, add 150 g of quark or Greek-style yoghurt. **[Expert opinion]**

### Plant protein counts

Cheap plant proteins are a big part of the budget plan, and the base of a vegetarian one, so it's worth knowing they work.

- **Soya is as good as animal protein for gains.** A meta-analysis of 9 trials (266 people) found soy protein gave similar gains in strength and lean mass to whey and other animal proteins with resistance training. **[Moderate evidence]**
- **Lentils and chana dal** carry less leucine and less of some essential amino acids than meat or dairy. Eat them with rice or chapati and alongside milk, eggs or yoghurt during the day, and hit your daily total. **[Expert opinion]**
- **What it costs:** soya mince and red lentils cost about 15–35p per 20 g of protein, against 60–95p for supermarket chicken breast. The full table is in [chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md).

> [!TIP]
> **The protein-density check.** Divide grams of protein by kcal and multiply by 100. A score of 10 or more (tuna, chicken, quark, skyr, soya mince) means a lean protein food. Between 5 and 10 (eggs, milk, Greek-style yoghurt, lentils) is a good all-rounder. Under 5 (peanut butter, nuts, oats) means a calorie food that brings some protein. The hub's food table shows this as **protein per 100 kcal** and can sort by it. **[Expert opinion]**

## Carbohydrate and fibre: fuel for training and hiking

Carbohydrate fuels hard sets and long stair-climber sessions, refills the glycogen in your muscles, and is the easiest way to eat a big calorie target. The BDA's ranges are **[Moderate evidence]**:

| Training load | Carbohydrate per kg a day | For the 80 kg example |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 hours of light to moderate activity, 3–5 times a week | 3–5 g | 240–400 g |
| 2–3 hours of intense activity a day, 5–6 times a week | 5–8 g | 400–640 g |

The programme's 45–90 minutes a day sits in the first band. The standard day gives roughly 390 g (about 5 g/kg for the 80 kg example), with most of it around training. On long-walk days, move towards **5–7 g/kg** with a bigger breakfast, fuel on the way by the long-walk rule below, and a proper meal afterwards. **[Expert opinion]**

**Fibre:** aim for **30 g a day**. The NHS says most adults manage only about 20 g. Oats, chickpeas, lentils, peas, wholemeal bread and fruit get you there. If appetite limits your gain, don't make every meal bulky: white rice and chapatis are fine. **[Expert opinion]**

## Fat

- Get about **20–35% of your calories from fat**: roughly 68–119 g a day at the male example's 3,060 kcal (Iraki and colleagues). **[Moderate evidence]**
- Choose mostly **unsaturated fats**: rapeseed or olive oil, peanuts, seeds and oily fish.
- **Oily fish:** the NHS recommends at least **2 portions of fish a week, including 1 oily**. A tin of sardines or mackerel is the budget way to do it. Tinned tuna doesn't count as oily. If you don't eat fish, see the omega-3 notes in [chapter 16](16-supplements.md).
- Fat is your **easiest calorie lever.** A tablespoon of oil or a heaped spoon of peanut butter adds about 120 kcal without making a meal bigger.

## Hydration

- The NHS suggests **6–8 cups or glasses of fluid a day**, more when you're active. Aim for **pale yellow urine.** Water, milk, tea and coffee all count.
- **During training:** Andy Galpin's rule of thumb works out at about **2 ml per kg every 15–20 minutes**: about 160 ml for the 80 kg example. Treat it as the upper end, for sweaty sessions. **[Expert opinion]**
- **On long hikes,** weigh yourself before and after. The American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) aims to keep sweat losses under **2% of body weight**, about 1.6 kg for the example. If you lose more, drink more next time. **[Moderate evidence]**
- **When you wake,** have a glass or two of water before training. **[Expert opinion]**

## Morning training: what to eat before and after

The programme is built for training first thing, and the meal templates assume waking at about 05:15–05:30, training from about 06:00 and having breakfast straight after. The usual advice of a light meal **2–3 hours** before exercise (BDA) isn't realistic at 6 am, so you have two options, both **[Expert opinion]**:

- **Option A: a small pre-gym snack (best for most sessions).** Eat 15–40 g of quick carbohydrate 20–40 minutes before you start: a banana and 2 dates (about 170 kcal, as in the standard day), or a slice of toast with honey. Use it before longer sessions, intervals and leg days.
- **Option B: train fasted.** Fine for easy sessions under about an hour, as long as you ate a proper dinner and a pre-bed snack. If you feel flat or light-headed, switch to Option A. Never train fasted before a long hike.

The ISSN says protein timing around training is largely personal preference, because a session's muscle-building effect lasts at least 24 hours. Your **daily total** counts most. **[Strong evidence]** Even so, eat breakfast within about an hour of finishing, with about 30 g of protein and plenty of carbohydrate. If you train later in the day, keep the same pattern: a small carbohydrate snack before, a proper meal after. Caffeine timing is in [chapter 16](16-supplements.md).

## Bone-healing nutrients: what's proven and what isn't

Your bone needs raw materials, but if you aren't short of something, extra hasn't been shown to speed healing. A 2022 systematic review of diet and fracture healing found "a dearth of evidence" that specific foods or supplements help humans. **[Moderate evidence]** Your aim: **no shortfalls and no megadoses.**

| Nutrient | What it does | UK daily need | Best sources | Does extra speed healing? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Protein** | Builds the collagen scaffold for new bone, and muscle | 1.6–2.2 g/kg (your target on the Nutrition page) | Milk, yoghurt, eggs, soya, lentils, fish, chicken | Shortfalls hurt. Extra is unproven |
| **Calcium** | Hardens bone | **700 mg** | Milk (250 ml is about 300 mg), yoghurt, sardines with bones (about 550 mg a tin), white bread | No, if you already get 700 mg |
| **Vitamin D** | Lets you absorb calcium | **10 µg**, October to March | A supplement in winter; oily fish and egg yolks | Mixed, mostly no (see below) |
| **Vitamin C** | Needed to make collagen and heal wounds | **40 mg** | An orange or a kiwi, peppers, potatoes | Not shown for bone |
| **Vitamin K** | Activates bone proteins, helps blood clot | About **1 µg per kg** | Spinach, broccoli, vegetable oils | No human evidence |
| **Zinc** | Helps make new cells and heal wounds | **9.5 mg** (men) or **7 mg** (women) | Meat, dairy, bread, chickpeas, pumpkin seeds | No human evidence |

Needs are from the NHS and BDA. Every meal template in chapter 15 gives at least 1,400 mg of calcium.

### Vitamin D: the honest version

- **Between October and early March the UK doesn't get enough sunlight to make vitamin D**, so the NHS says everyone should consider **10 µg a day** in autumn and winter. If you have dark skin, for example an African, African-Caribbean or South Asian background, or you're rarely outdoors, it advises considering it **all year**. **[Strong evidence]** If it's between October and March, start now.
- **Don't take more than 100 µg (4,000 IU) a day** unless a doctor prescribes it. Too much makes calcium build up, which can weaken bones and harm the kidneys and heart (NHS).
- **Does extra speed healing?** A systematic review called the human data "too inconsistent". A small trial in wrist fractures found that about 700 IU a day "does not improve distal radius fracture healing", and that about 1,800 IU a day "may be detrimental". So there's **[Moderate evidence]** that routine extra doses don't help if you aren't deficient. Ask your GP or clinic whether a blood test makes sense.
- Gelatine-free forms (halal-friendly and vegetarian) and costs are in [chapter 16](16-supplements.md).

### Calcium, vitamin C and vitamin A

- **Calcium, food first.** Two or three servings of milk or yoghurt a day cover it, and a tin of sardines covers most of a day on its own. Spinach doesn't count, because you absorb little of its calcium.
- **Vitamin C.** You may read that vitamin C prevents complex regional pain syndrome (CRPS) after wrist fractures. In one large trial, 500 mg a day **for 50 days**, started soon after a wrist fracture, cut CRPS from 10.1% to 1.8%. **[Moderate evidence]** It was started within days of the fracture, so whether it's still relevant to you, and whether it applies to your fracture, is a question for your clinic. Day to day, one orange gives about twice the 40 mg you need.
- **Vitamin A.** High intakes over many years may weaken bones. Keep your total at **1.5 mg a day or less**: no liver more than once a week, no fish-liver oils and no high-dose vitamin A supplements (NHS). **[Moderate evidence]**

> [!SAFETY]
> Show your fracture clinic **every** supplement and medicine you take, including any "bone", "joint" or "healing" products. Ask before you take anti-inflammatories such as ibuprofen for training soreness ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)).

## If you eat halal: budget principles

This section is optional. The meal plans are halal-friendly by design, and the swaps are in [chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md).

- **Many of the cheapest proteins are halal by nature.** Milk, yoghurt, eggs, lentils, chickpeas, soya mince and tinned fish need no certificate. Only meat does.
- **Buy halal meat where it's cheapest per kilo,** often a halal butcher's bulk box frozen in 100 g portions, and stretch it with lentils or soya mince.
- **Four label checks** cover almost everything: gelatine, rennet in cheese and whey, additives and alcohol-based flavourings, and meat-based stock cubes and ready meals. Chapter 15 explains each one and how the UK certification bodies differ; chapter 16 covers supplements and capsules.
- **Some choices are personal.** Muslims differ on cheese rennet, stunned meat, prawns and shellfish, and trace alcohol in flavourings. This guide flags each one and leaves the decision to you.

## If you're vegetarian

This section is optional too.

- **The plans already lean on dairy, eggs, pulses and soya**, so going meat-free costs nothing. Chapter 15 has a vegetarian swap for every meat and fish meal, with the change in calories and protein.
- **Protein is easy without meat.** Soya is as good as animal protein for gains (above), and milk, yoghurt and eggs are rich in leucine.
- **Without fish,** you don't need omega-3 for muscle; an algae-oil capsule is the option if you want one ([chapter 16](16-supplements.md)).
- **Check labels for gelatine and animal rennet** (chapter 15's four label checks).
- **If you're vegan,** use a calcium-fortified soya drink and soya yoghurt in place of dairy, and make sure you have a reliable source of vitamin B12 (NHS; [chapter 16](16-supplements.md)).

## Fasting periods

Some people fast for religious reasons, for example in Ramadan or during other religious fasts, and some plan a fast for other reasons. This section covers how the hub handles any fasting period, then the details for a dawn-to-sunset fast such as Ramadan. If you don't fast, skip it.

**Whether to fast while you're recovering** is your decision. Many faiths make allowances for illness and recovery; if you're unsure, talk to your doctor and, if you wish, a religious adviser you trust. If you take regular medicines, ask your GP or pharmacist how to time them. If it's a planned fast for another reason, check it with your GP or fracture clinic first, and remember there's no dieting while the bone heals: a fast is for holding your weight, not losing it.

### Tell the hub: Settings → Fasting periods

Add each fast under **Settings → Fasting periods**: a name, the first and last day, and whether the dates are confirmed yet. Religious dates often depend on the moon, so correct them once they're announced. On every date inside a fasting period the hub:

- **applies easy-day mode automatically**, exactly as on an AMBER morning: half the sets, Zone 2 only, no intervals, same loads ("Hold: same load, RIR 3–4"). It never suggests adding load or reps on those days;
- shows a fasting reminder: train after you break your fast if you can, stop at any dizziness or light-headedness, and no new lifts this period;
- **pauses the weight verdict** for the fasting period and the 14 days after it, so there's no nudge to gain while you hold steady or while your weight settles afterwards;
- schedules no tests: they wait until the fast is over;
- leaves your phase dates where they are. A fasting period pauses progress; it doesn't move the programme.

RED still means no training, fasting or not. Expect shorter sleep too: a 2025 meta-analysis found sleep duration and efficiency fell during Ramadan, especially in amateur athletes **[Moderate evidence]** ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)).

### Training: after you break your fast, if you can

- **Best:** train **1–2 hours after you break your fast** (iftar, in Ramadan), once you've had dates or another quick carbohydrate, water and a light meal, so you can drink during the session. Finish early enough that your bedtime doesn't slip.
- **If evenings don't work,** keep the session to the easy-day version, shorter if you like, and skip anything that leaves you breathless or sweaty, especially if your fast means you can't drink until it ends.
- **No new lifts and no tests** until the fast is over. Fasted training feels harder; that's expected, not a sign you're losing fitness. **[Expert opinion]**

> [!SAFETY]
> **The dizziness stop rule.** Stop at the first sign of dizziness or light-headedness, blurred vision, nausea or a pounding heart. On a treadmill or stair climber, pull the safety key or press stop and keep your uninjured hand on the rail until it has stopped. Then sit down straight away, on a bench or the floor, and lie with your legs raised if it doesn't pass. Don't train again that day. If it doesn't settle within a few minutes, or you faint or have chest pain, get medical help (999 for chest pain or fainting). If it happens more than once, see your GP before training fasted again. A fall onto your healing arm is what this rule prevents. **[Expert opinion]**

### Protein, fluids and weight across your eating window

- **Hold your weight steady:** don't aim to gain during a fast, and never let it drop. That means eating about your maintenance (the Nutrition page shows it; for the male example, about 2,760 kcal), with protein still at your target. If you're still below your pre-injury weight, the fast pauses the regain; it doesn't undo it. The hub's calorie target doesn't change for fasting periods and it won't nudge you to gain, so judge the period by your 7-day average yourself: if it falls, add one of [chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md)'s add-ons. **[Expert opinion]**
- **Protein in three anchors:** about 30–40 g when you break the fast and at the meal that follows it, 40 g or more at the **meal after training**, and 30–40 g at the **pre-dawn meal** (suhoor, in Ramadan: overnight oats with yoghurt, or eggs). A yoghurt or milky snack before bed tops it up. **[Expert opinion]**
- **Fluids:** if your fast includes water, spread about **2–2.5 litres** across your eating window: 2 glasses when you break the fast, 1–2 with each meal, sips through training and 2 before dawn. Milk counts. Keep tea and coffee to early evening, because late caffeine cuts an already short night. **[Expert opinion]**
- **Keep the basics and you keep your fitness.** A 2025 review of 31 studies concluded that athletes who kept up their energy and macronutrient intake, training load, sleep and body composition were unlikely to see substantial drops in performance during Ramadan. **[Moderate evidence]** [Chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md) has a sample fasting day built from the batch cooking.

### After a fast: 14 quiet days

- **Go straight back to your normal day** (or the step day you were on) the day after the fast ends, pre-gym snack and full protein spread included.
- **Expect your weight to jump** in the first days back, as normal meals, fluids and glycogen return. That's water and food, not fat, and it's why the hub gives **no weight verdict for the 14 days after your fasting period ends**. **[Expert opinion]**
- **Don't chase a catch-up gain.** Your band carries on where it was. The first verdict after a fast comes about a month after it ends, once a whole fortnight sits clear of those 14 days.
- **Tests and new lifts return** once the fasting period is over, and the hub schedules them as normal.

## Alcohol

Alcohol disrupts sleep, blunts muscle building and worsens balance, and falls are your biggest risk while the bone heals. If you drink at all, keep it small, and never the night before a long hike or a hard session. **[Expert opinion]**

## Smoking and vaping

This matters more for your arm than almost anything else in this chapter.

- **Smoking roughly doubles the risk of a fracture not healing.** A systematic review found an adjusted odds ratio of **2.32** for nonunion (the bone failing to join) in smokers, and **1.95** for open fractures. **[Strong evidence]**
- After wrist fracture surgery, smokers had more complications than non-smokers: **9.8%** against **5.6%**. **[Moderate evidence]**
- **Vaping:** the NHS says it's likely to be far less harmful than smoking, but "not completely risk-free" and "not recommended for non-smokers". Nicotine itself harms bone cells and fracture repair in lab and animal studies, and nobody has tested vaping in human fracture healing yet. **[Expert opinion]**

**What to do:** if you smoke, stopping now is one of the best things you can do for your arm. If you can't stop yet, switching completely to a vape beats smoking, and less nicotine is better still. The NHS Quit Smoking app and your local Stop Smoking Service are free.

## Appetite tips for a big target

These tips are **[Expert opinion]**.

1. **Eat by the clock, not by hunger.** Seven eating times keep each meal manageable.
2. **Drink some calories:** milk, or the home-made milkshake in chapter 15 (about 530 kcal).
3. **Add energy-dense extras:** oil in cooking, peanut butter, peanuts, raisins and dates.
4. **Eat protein first and salad last.**
5. **Drink water between meals**, not a pint just before.
6. **Repeat meals you like**, and batch-cook twice a week.
7. **Keep snacks visible:** peanuts in your bag, boiled eggs at eye level in the fridge.
8. **Log calories and protein for two weeks** in the hub's check-in, then just spot-check.

> [!WARNING]
> Losing weight without trying, losing your appetite, or feeling feverish or unwell isn't a nutrition problem. After a fracture, especially an open fracture or surgery, it can be a sign of infection. Treat it as a red flag and contact the fracture clinic or NHS 111 (see [chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md)).

## Eating on long walks: the one fuelling rule

Chapters [11](11-hiking-training.md) and [13](13-uk-hiking-roadmap.md) build your walks and hikes up gradually. This is the single fuelling rule for all of them; other chapters link here.

> [!TIP]
> **The long-walk rule.** If a walk will last more than an hour, **start eating at about 60 minutes**, then keep going little and often: a few bites every 20–30 minutes, about **30 g of carbohydrate an hour**, rising towards **60 g an hour** on walks over 2 hours or hilly days. Under an hour, water is enough.

- **Why these amounts:** endurance research supports up to about 30 g an hour for 1–2-hour efforts and about 60 g for 2–3 hours, and Jeukendrup notes intake can come down at low intensity, which walking is. **[Moderate evidence]** for endurance sport, **[Expert opinion]** for walking.
- **Before:** a carbohydrate-rich breakfast 2–3 hours before you set off, such as porridge with a banana.
- **About 25–40 g of carbohydrate** is a large banana, 2 slices of malt loaf, about 5 dates, a 60 g flapjack or 50 g of jelly sweets (gelatine-free versions, labelled vegan or halal, if you avoid gelatine).
- **Protein and salt:** on walks over about 2 hours, add a little protein at lunch (a tuna wrap, or jerky), and salty snacks such as salted peanuts on long or sweaty days ([chapter 16](16-supplements.md) covers electrolytes). **[Expert opinion]**
- **Afterwards:** a proper meal with protein and carbohydrate within 1–2 hours, and eat more that day and the next. Long-walk days use the 3,300 kcal step-up day ([chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md)).

> [!SAFETY]
> **One-handed walking food.** Everything must open with one hand: pre-open packets, cut wraps in half and use zip bags. Until your arm reaches Light use / rehab started, you carry a **waist pack only (2 kg or less)**, so take a flip-cap bottle and a few snacks in it; a day pack with a hydration bladder waits for that gate ([chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md) has the carrying rule). While the bone heals, keep to good paths, and stop on stable ground to eat rather than wrestling a wrapper on the move.

## Reading UK food labels

- **Compare foods per 100 g, not per portion.** Manufacturers choose their own portion sizes (NHS).
- **Use the unit price on the shelf label** (price per kg or per litre) to compare packs. Chapter 15 shows how to work out the cost per 20 g of protein.
- **Protein claims:** "source of protein" means at least 12% of the food's energy comes from protein, and "high protein" at least 20%. A "high protein" cookie can still be mostly sugar and fat, so use the protein-density check.
- **%RI figures** assume 2,000 kcal a day. Your target is probably higher (about 3,060 kcal for the male example), so the kcal %RI isn't your target. The 6 g salt reference still applies.
- **Label checks for halal and vegetarian diets** (gelatine, rennet, additives and flavourings) are in [chapter 15](15-meal-plans.md).

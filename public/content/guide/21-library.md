# The Library — Books, Podcasts, Channels, Kit

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - A short, checked list of books, podcasts, channels, apps and kit. Each entry says why it matters to you and where to start.
> - **Budget first:** borrow books and audiobooks free with a UK public library card (many libraries use apps such as BorrowBox or Libby), and listen to podcasts free in any podcast app.
> - Start with three books: *Outlive* (why you're training), *Hill Walking* (how to move safely in the hills) and *Atomic Habits* or *Tiny Habits* (how to stay consistent).
> - For podcasts, start with Andy Galpin's six-part Huberman Lab series, ideally during Zone 2 sessions.
> - **Kit:** a scale (a smart scale is fine) and a tape measure now, and a chest-strap heart-rate monitor if you want accurate Zone 2 numbers. Legal copies only: no pirated PDFs.

## How to use this library

- **One book at a time,** read for principles, not protocols.
- **One idea a week,** written in your **Weekly review**.
- **Gates win.** If anything here conflicts with your gates or your physio, the gates and the physio win. Every time.
- **"(not verified)"** marks details without a confirmed primary source.

## Free and cheap: borrowing and listening

- **Get a library card.** UK public libraries lend print books, and many also lend e-books and e-audiobooks free through apps such as BorrowBox or Libby, with magazines and newspapers on apps such as PressReader (not verified for every library). Check what your local library offers on its website, or ask at the desk.
- **Reserve, don't buy.** If your library doesn't have a book, ask whether you can reserve it or request it (not verified for every library).
- **Podcasts are free** in any podcast app, and most of the episodes below are also free on YouTube.
- **Buy only what you'll reread,** such as a navigation handbook you'll carry on the hill.

## Books

### Start here

| Book | Author(s) | Why you're reading it | Start with |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Outlive: The Science and Art of Longevity* (2023) | Peter Attia with Bill Gifford | The *why* behind your training: healthspan, the four pillars and the Centenarian Decathlon | The exercise chapters (chapter numbers not verified) |
| *Hill Walking* (3rd edition) | Steve Long, for Mountain Training | Mountain Training's handbook for hill walkers: navigation, weather, hazards and incidents | "Movement and route finding", "Navigation tools" and "The weather" |
| *Atomic Habits*, or *Tiny Habits* (2019) | James Clear / BJ Fogg | How to make training automatic. Pick one, not both | Clear on systems and identity; Fogg on starting tiny |

### Strength

- ***Kettlebell Simple & Sinister*** — Pavel Tsatsouline (StrongFirst, 2013). The clearest statement of "practice, not workout". Read the philosophy now; train the programme only after Full clearance, because swings and get-ups need it.
- ***The Naked Warrior*** — Pavel Tsatsouline (2003). Bodyweight strength built on tension skills and greasing the groove. The one-arm push-up and pistol work wait for Full clearance.
- ***Easy Strength*** — Dan John and Pavel Tsatsouline (2011). Strength practice that leaves energy for everything else. For later.

### Muscle

- ***Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy*** — Brad Schoenfeld (Human Kinetics; first edition 2016, with later editions). The textbook on how muscle grows: dense, so dip in. Pair it with [chapter 08](08-building-muscle.md).

### Hills, navigation and mountain endurance

- ***Navigation in the Mountains*** — Carlo Forte (Mountain Training, 2012). The official navigation handbook for Mountain Training's mountain qualifications. Start with "The fundamentals" and "Navigation techniques".
- ***Training for the Uphill Athlete*** — Steve House, Scott Johnston and Kílian Jornet (Patagonia, 2019). Endurance training for mountain sports, built on a big aerobic base: the thinking behind your Zone 2 work. Pair it with [chapters 10](10-engine-cardio.md) and [11](11-hiking-training.md).

### Habits and mindset

- ***Atomic Habits*** — James Clear. Systems over goals, and identity-based habits.
- ***Tiny Habits*** — BJ Fogg (2019). Tiny behaviours attached to routines you already have.
- ***Discipline Equals Freedom: Field Manual*** (2017) and ***Extreme Ownership*** (2015, with Leif Babin) — Jocko Willink. Short and blunt. Read them with the cautions in [chapter 02](02-philosophy.md): discipline should protect your sleep, not cut it.
- ***Can't Hurt Me*** (2018) — David Goggins, and ***Endure*** (2022) — Cameron Hanes. Resilience and work ethic. Inspiration, never a training plan, and especially not for a healing arm.
- ***Jailhouse Strong*** — Josh Bryant and Adam benShea (2013). Strength methods that adapt to the person and the setting.

## Kit

### Start with what you have

- **A scale:** any bathroom scale works for the daily weigh-in and weight trend. If it's a smart scale, ignore its body-fat figures ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)).
- **A wearable, if you have one:** fine for sleep and step trends. If you stop using it, export your data first, and if you change how you measure resting HR, press "I changed how I measure resting HR" in Settings ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)).

### Worth buying now (no clearance needed)

| Item | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chest-strap heart-rate monitor | Accurate heart rate for Zone 2 and intervals | In a study comparing devices with an ECG, the chest strap was the most accurate, and wrist devices varied with the activity. **[Moderate evidence]** Example: the Polar H10 (£69.20 on Polar UK when checked in November 2026; Bluetooth and ANT+). Cheaper straps from other brands exist (not verified) |
| Flexible tape measure | Waist and thigh measurements | A self-retracting body tape is easier one-handed **[Expert opinion]** |
| Water bottle with a flip lid, and a light gym bag or waist pack | Habit and hydration | A bottle you can open with your uninjured hand. The bag follows the carrying rule in [chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md): 3 kg or less, on your uninjured shoulder |
| Trainers with flat, stable soles | Safer on machines and for balance work | Nothing with a high, squashy heel |

> [!TIP]
> A chest strap is fiddly to fasten one-handed. Set it once to a snug loop, then slip it over your head and slide it into place with your uninjured hand ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)). Never hold or pull the strap with your injured hand.

### Later

- **Digital hand-grip dynamometer:** tracks your uninjured hand's grip from Good arm OK, logged on **Your arm**. Measure your injured hand only as your physio directs. Models and prices (not verified).
- **Hiking basics:** the full list is in [chapter 12](12-hiking-skills-and-kit.md). Trekking poles need grip, so they wait for Grip cleared.
- **Kettlebells:** for *Simple & Sinister* after Full clearance, if your gym doesn't have them.

## An optional 12-week reading and listening plan

| Weeks | Read | Listen (during Zone 2) |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | *Atomic Habits* or *Tiny Habits* (borrowed) | Galpin series, episodes 1 and 4 |
| 5–8 | *Outlive*: the exercise chapters | Drive #206 and #261, and Galpin episode 5 (recovery) |
| 9–12 | *Hill Walking*: the navigation and weather chapters | Uphill Athlete "80/20" episode, and Galpin episode 3 (endurance) |
| Then | *Navigation in the Mountains*, then *Simple & Sinister* (for later) | Pavel on Huberman Lab, and JRE episodes as you like |

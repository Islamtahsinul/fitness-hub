# Your UK Hiking Roadmap

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - Ten levels take you from today's walks to multi-day trails over all terrains. The Hikes page holds every level's target, gate and example routes.
> - **Levels 1–3 are open now** (no clearance needed): tarmac or firm, level gravel, with a waist pack only. Level 4 adds a light day pack at Light use / rehab started. **Levels 5–10 all need Full clearance.**
> - Your Saturday long walk is capped at 80 minutes until Phase 4. Level 3 and longer walks are optional extras for a free weekend, on GREEN days only.
> - Your first 8 weekends are planned below by programme week, as flat, firm walks you can find near you. The Saturday card and the Hikes page show each week's walk.
> - Each mission is tied to the earliest phase it can happen in: a Level 3–4 day in Phases 1–3, a first mountain from the end of Phase 4, and a challenge day and the West Highland Way from Phase 5.

## How the ladder works

Each level has a **gate**, a **target walk** (distance, ascent, pack weight and terrain) and UK **example routes**, all on the Hikes page. Log each hike against a ladder level: the hub ticks a level the first time a logged hike meets its numbers while its gate is open. The gates are cumulative, so once a higher gate is cleared, everything below it is open too.

### When to move up

Move up a level when **all** of these are true:

1. **You've done the target walk twice** in the last 4–6 weeks, on different days.
2. **It felt comfortable.** You spent most of the walk in Zone 1–2, finished at an RPE of 6/10 or less, and could have kept going for another hour.
3. **You recovered normally.** Next-day soreness was 3/10 or less, and your morning resting HR was back to normal within 48 hours.
4. **The arm stayed quiet.** No red flags, and no rise in arm pain after the walk.
5. **The next level's gate is cleared** in the hub.
6. **You have the skills and kit for the next level.** For example, map and compass before Level 7, and a winter skills course before any snow.

**Move down a level** after illness, a niggle, a fall, a long break, or when the season turns (winter conditions make every hill one level harder). There's no shame in it. Pros do it constantly **[Expert opinion]**.

## Levels 1–4: what's open before full clearance

This is your whole outdoor world until the clinic clears more: **tarmac or firm, level gravel only**, dry and stile-free. Riverside walkways, park paths and old railway lines are ideal. If the surface turns to grass, mud, roots, loose stones, steps or a steep slope, turn back, because that ground waits for Full clearance. The rules from [Hiking Skills, Kit & Mountain Safety](12-hiking-skills-and-kit.md) apply too: daylight, hands free, and no ice.

**Finding Level 1–4 routes near you.** Park paths, riverside walkways, surfaced towpaths and old railway lines are the best bets. Accessible-route lists are the quickest way to find surfaced, step-free ground: the Lake District's "Miles without Stiles" routes graded "For all", Forestry and Land Scotland trails graded Easy (most accessible), traffic-free sections of the National Cycle Network on old railway lines (shared with cyclists), and the accessible routes on your council's, country park's or national park's website. Check the surface on the route's own page, and turn back if the ground doesn't match it.

> [!SAFETY]
> Levels 1–3 mean **tarmac or firm, level gravel only**: no mud, steep ramps, steps, roots or rocky ground. Busy paths are fine if they're paved. Turn back if the surface changes, and use the treadmill instead if it's icy, frosty or covered in wet leaves. On shared paths, keep your injured arm on the side away from passing bikes where you can, and step aside and stop when they pass **[Expert opinion]**.

**Level 3 and longer walks are optional extras.** The programme caps your Saturday long walk at 80 minutes until Phase 4, and a Level 3 walk takes about two hours or more. So treat Level 3, and Level 4 later, as an extra for a free weekend, within the readiness rule and the programme's limits:
- Do it only on a GREEN day, never in a deload week, and in place of that week's long walk, not as well as it.
- Grow your longest walk by about 15–30 minutes at a time, and keep it in Zone 2.
- Stay on the firm, level ground that's open now, carrying only what [the carrying rule](18-arm-recovery.md#the-carrying-rule) allows: a waist pack (2 kg or less) until Light use / rehab started.
- Fuel it by the one long-walk rule in [Nutrition](14-nutrition.md).
- Log it under Log a session as that day's long walk. The hub then offers to add it to your hike log, where it counts towards the ladder.

**Level 4 (Light use / rehab started)** keeps the same firm, level terrain and adds a light day pack: 2–4 kg to start, adding about 1 kg every 2–3 weeks as long as the walk and the next morning feel fine, put on sitting down, uninjured arm first. From Grip cleared you can add poles, but the terrain stays the same.

**Not yet:** some popular "easy" walks have sections that wait for Full clearance. Tarn Hows (graded "For some") reaches 1:5 in places, the Loch Morlich Trail has muddy patches and a steep ramp, the Buttermere lakeshore path is currently "challenging" because of washouts and large stones, and canal towpaths such as the Monmouthshire & Brecon can be muddy. Of the Lake District's 50 stile-free "Miles without Stiles" routes, only the **"For all"** grade fits your gate: tarmac or smooth compacted stone, no steeper than 1:10.

## Levels 5–10: after full clearance

Everything from Level 5 needs Full clearance, and even then the programme's 4-week Phase 4a bridge brings in rough paths and hills before any big day. The Hikes page lists each level's routes, with distances, terrain notes and links. In short:
- **Level 5, gentle hills with poles:** chalk downs such as the North Downs Way over Box Hill, the Ridgeway from Wendover to Ivinghoe Beacon and the South Downs circulars from Lewes and Amberley, or a day section of the Yorkshire Wolds Way.
- **Level 6, first mountains on made paths:** Pen-y-ghent, Yr Wyddfa by the Llanberis Path, and Mam Tor and the Great Ridge.
- **Level 7, rough ground:** Kinder Scout from Edale, Helvellyn from Thirlmere and the Pyg Track. Stay off Crib Goch.
- **Level 8, big days:** the Yorkshire Three Peaks, Ben Nevis, and Helvellyn via Striding Edge after a scrambling course.
- **Levels 9–10, long trails:** the Thames Path and the South Downs, North Downs and Ridgeway National Trails in the south; the West Highland Way, Hadrian's Wall Path and Great Glen Way in the north; the Pennine Way and Coast to Coast as multi-week goals.

## Seasons: the UK hiking calendar

| Months | What it's like | For you |
|---|---|---|
| **Dec–Feb** | The shortest days: under 8 hours of daylight at the solstice in southern England, and about 6 h 45 min in Fort William. Frost, ice and snow on the hills | Before Full clearance, Levels 1–3 on dry, ice-free days only. Use the treadmill when it's frosty |
| **Mar–Apr** | Lengthening days, but snow lingers on the high tops. Lambing starts | Build distance on firm, level trails |
| **May–Jun** | The best walking months. May is the most popular month on the West Highland Way, and Fort William has about 17 h 50 min of daylight at midsummer. Midges arrive in Scotland | Mission season |
| **Jul–Aug** | Warm and busy. Midges are usually worst in the Scottish Highlands from June until August | Go early or midweek. Carry midge repellent and a head net in Scotland |
| **Sep–Oct** | Often settled and quieter, with shortening days. Deer stalking is busiest in the Scottish hills, especially the first three weeks of October | Big-day season. Check Heading for the Scottish Hills before walking in deer forest |
| **Nov** | Dark by about 4pm, with the first snow on the tops | Back to the treadmill and the gym |

Winter conditions turn any UK mountain into a Level 8+ day that needs ice axe and crampon skills, and snow can lie on Ben Nevis into early summer.

## Logistics

- **Getting there by public transport:** many ladder routes start at or near a railway station, including Edale (Kinder Scout and the start of the Pennine Way) and Horton-in-Ribblesdale (Pen-y-ghent and the Three Peaks). Check journey times and weekend engineering works before you go, and treat far-off bases as an overnight.
- **Booking:** book multi-day accommodation early (the West Highland Way advises booking "year-round"), and book Advance train tickets early for long journeys.
- **Baggage transfer:** the West Highland Way has a directory of baggage-transfer services, and Speyside Way taxi firms move bags too. That's what makes Level 9 possible before you're ready to carry 12 kg.
- **Camping:** in Scotland, wild camping is part of access rights under the Scottish Outdoor Access Code (the West Highland Way's guidance is "two or three nights in any one place"), but Loch Lomond & The Trossachs byelaws apply from March to September. The Cotswold Way's own page says wild camping is not legal there, and in most of England and Wales you should assume you need the landowner's permission (not verified for each area).
- **The hub:** log every hike with distance, ascent, time, pack weight, average HR, RPE and ladder level. Your progress graph is your evidence for moving up.

## Your missions

These are targets, not promises, and each one is tied to the earliest phase it can happen in. Phase dates follow your gates: Phase 1 runs at least 4 weeks, Phases 2a–3 take about 20 weeks once Grip cleared is set, and the 4-week Phase 4a bridge can only start after Full clearance. Even if every gate came at the earliest possible moment, Phase 4a couldn't start before programme week 26, Phase 4 before week 30, or Phase 5 before week 46, about ten months after you start.

### Mission 1: your first big day (Phases 1–3)

| What you have by then | Mission | Earliest phase |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing beyond Good arm OK | **A Level 3 day:** 8–10 km on a flat, firm route, such as the whole Fallowfield Loop (8.7 km, Chorlton-cum-Hardy to Fairfield), an out-and-back on the Monsal Trail, the Keswick Railway Path or the Loch Leven Heritage Trail, or a long riverside walkway, as a free-weekend extra | Phase 1, once you've done Level 2 twice comfortably (from about programme week 10) |
| Light use / rehab started or Grip cleared | **A Level 4 day with a light pack:** the Cuckoo Trail from Polegate (14–17.5 km), the Mawddach or Monsal Trail end to end, or 13–14 km of the Bristol & Bath Railway Path or the Camel Trail. Poles from Grip cleared, 70% grip and your physio's OK | Phase 1 once Light use / rehab started is set and Level 3 has gone well twice; most likely in Phases 2–3 |
| Full clearance | **A Level 4 day, then your first rough paths.** Phase 4a's Saturdays bring in rough paths and hills first; walks on Level 5 routes follow as your Phase 4 long day grows | Level 5 routes: Phase 4, from programme week 30 at the earliest |

**Build-up:** finish Phase 1 and work up through the ladder levels your gates allow. Once you have Full clearance, book Mountain Training's Hill Skills course if you can.

### Mission 2: a big mountain day (the end of Phase 4 at the earliest; more likely Phase 5)

- **The aim:** Yr Wyddfa by the Llanberis Path (14.5 km, 975 m, about 7 hours), with Pen-y-ghent (9.4 km, about 2.5 hours) as your first mountain.
- **Earliest phase:** Phase 4, after Full clearance and the 4-week Phase 4a bridge, and only once Level 5 has gone comfortably twice. A Level 5 day (about 18 km and 600 m) takes 5–6 hours, and your weekend long day only reaches 5 hours in Phase 4's benchmark week (its week 16), so your first mountains come at the very end of Phase 4 or in Phase 5: about programme weeks 45–46 with the earliest possible gates. Mountain days want a clear, calm day between May and September, so if that point lands in autumn or winter, aim for the next May–September.
- **On the day:** go with someone, on a clear, calm day, with a turn-around time ([chapter 12](12-hiking-skills-and-kit.md)).

### Mission 3: a challenge day, then your first long trail (Phase 5)

- **A Level 8 day:** the Yorkshire Three Peaks (38.6 km, about 1,600 m) or the Ben Nevis Mountain Track (about 17 km and 1,350 m, not verified). **Earliest phase:** Phase 5, the Mission phase, once Levels 6 and 7 are behind you, using the 12-week "first big hike" block in [chapter 11](11-hiking-training.md). Phase 5 can't start before programme week 46, and both routes are spring-to-early-autumn days, so plan for the first summer after you reach Phase 5 with Levels 6 and 7 done. Helvellyn via Striding Edge counts too, but only after a scrambling or Mountain Skills course.
- **The West Highland Way (Level 9):** 96 miles (154 km) over 6–7 days with baggage transfer. **Earliest phase:** Phase 5, after your Level 8 days. That makes September of the summer you do them the earliest realistic window, and the following May–June the classic one: May is the trail's most popular month, with long daylight, and comes before the worst of the midges.
  - **Prepare:** run the 12-week block with back-to-back weekend days from week 5, do a rehearsal of two or three consecutive days with your pack 4–6 weeks before, and book accommodation and baggage transfer over the winter.
  - **Alternatives:** Hadrian's Wall Path (84 miles) or the Great Glen Way (75–79 miles).

**After that (Level 10):** when you have Levels 7–9 behind you and you've trained with a 10–15 kg pack, the Coast to Coast or the Pennine Way become realistic multi-week goals. Before any winter hill day, do a winter skills course (Mountaineering Scotland runs them through the winter), if the arm and your physio say yes.

## Your first 8 weekends (Levels 1–2)

These follow the hub's Saturday long walk for programme weeks 1–8: the Re-entry Week, then your first seven weeks of Phase 1. The hub keys each walk to the programme week, so the Saturday card and the Hikes page show the right one whichever Monday you started. The walks are deliberately generic: each week, pick a flat, firm route near you that fits the description. If the hub's long walk shows a different time in any week, follow the hub; the route still works. Walk in Zone 2, start late morning once any frost has gone, and finish well before dusk **[Expert opinion]**. If it's icy, frosty, very wet or leafy, do the minutes on the treadmill instead, as the programme says.

| Programme week | Walk | Level | What to look for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (Re-entry Week) | 40 min | 1 | A flat 3 km park loop or firm towpath near you: 20 minutes out, then back |
| 2 | 45 min | 1 | A flat 3–4 km route on pavements or a paved riverside path. Go early, before the crowds |
| 3 | 60 min | 2 | Loops of a large park on its tarmac paths |
| 4 | 70 min | 2 | A longer loop on hard, step-free paths in a country park or along a river |
| 5 | 80 min | 2 | A full flat loop of about 6–7 km on tarmac or firm gravel |
| 6 (deload) | 56 min | 1 | A shorter flat route of about 4 km, at an easy pace |
| 7 | 80 min | 2 | A surfaced, stile-free trail a short journey from home, graded for all abilities |
| 8 | 80 min | 2 | An old railway path or firm towpath: 40 minutes out, 40 back |

**After weekend 8:** your first Level 3 is 8–10 km on the same kind of flat, firm ground, such as the whole length of an old railway path, as a free-weekend extra once you've done Level 2 twice, comfortably and with a quiet arm.

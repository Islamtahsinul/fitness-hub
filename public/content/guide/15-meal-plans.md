# Meal Plans, Shopping & Prep

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - The **standard day** is about **3,000 kcal and 149 g of protein** for roughly **£5 a day**. A **no-meat day** has the same calories, and two step days (about 2,700 and 3,300 kcal) are there for adjusting. The hub marks the one closest to your target.
> - **Halal-friendly and vegetarian swaps are built in.** The plans lean on foods that need no certificate (dairy, eggs, pulses, soya and fish), and every meat and fish meal has a swap.
> - The cheapest protein per 20 g is **soya mince, lentils, oats, milk, peanut butter, chickpeas, tinned tuna and own-brand Greek-style yoghurt**. Whey is a convenience, not a bargain.
> - **Batch-cook twice a week** (Sunday and Wednesday): a chicken or soya curry, soya keema, dal and boiled eggs. Eat leftovers within 2 days or freeze them, and eat cooked rice within 24 hours (FSA).
> - Cook **one-handed and safely**: frozen chopped veg, ring-pull tins, a microwave jug and a non-slip mat. Never carry or tip a hot pan with one hand, and get the big shop delivered ([chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md) has the carrying rule).

## How to use these plans

1. The hub's **Nutrition** page marks the template closest to your target. For the example profile (a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult), that's the **standard day** for a man (target about 3,060 kcal) and the **step-down day** for a woman (target about 2,800 kcal).
2. Eat it most days, swapping in the no-meat day, the swaps below, or the lunches and dinners below for variety.
3. Every fortnight the hub gives one weight verdict against your band ([chapter 14](14-nutrition.md) owns the bands). Fine-tune with the add-ons below after one verdict, and move up or down a whole step only after two in a row.
4. **Eat the same on rest days,** minus the pre-gym dates. Muscle and bone build on rest days too. **[Expert opinion]**
5. **On long-walk days, use the 3,300 kcal day,** and fuel the walk itself by the long-walk rule in [chapter 14](14-nutrition.md): start eating at about 60 minutes.
6. The hub stores all four days as templates, and your check-in can fill in calories and protein from the one you followed. Values are typical, so check labels.
7. **If you fast** (for example in Ramadan), use the fasting day below.
8. **Outside 2,700–3,300 kcal?** Below about 2,700, start from the step-down day and trim the carbohydrate and fat foods (rice, chapatis, peanuts, peanut butter, oil) until it matches your target, keeping the protein foods, and never go below the maintenance figure on your Nutrition page. Above 3,300, add from the add-ons table.

### Step down: 2,715 kcal, 131 g protein

The standard day without the dates and the 15:30 eggs and toast: banana (95 kcal, 1 g), porridge (690, 27 g), peanuts and an apple (240, 9 g), curry and rice (740, 46 g), keema with chapatis and raita (675, 33 g), and milk with wheat biscuits (275, 15 g).

**Check it against your maintenance first.** For the female example (maintenance about 2,500 kcal) it's still a surplus. For the male example (maintenance about 2,760 kcal) it's roughly maintenance, so instead of stepping down, take 100–150 kcal off the standard day with the cuts below. It's never a diet: if your weight trend goes flat or drops, go straight back up.

### Step up: 3,320 kcal, 152 g protein

The standard day with a **banana and peanut butter milkshake** at 15:30 instead of the eggs and toast: 300 ml whole milk, 1 banana, 20 g peanut butter and 30 g oats, blitzed in a one-button blender (530 kcal, 20 g protein). It costs roughly 55p, far less than a shop-bought "mass gainer".

### If you fast: a sample fasting day, 2,700 kcal and about 140 g protein

## Swaps: vegetarian and halal-friendly

Every template is built mostly from foods that need no halal certificate: milk, yoghurt, eggs, pulses, soya and fish. The only meat is chicken (and mince, if you make the chilli), so **if you eat halal, buy halal-certified chicken and mince**, and run the four label checks below on yoghurt, cheese, sweets and stock cubes. For a vegetarian day, use these swaps. The changes are per portion, so a day's totals move by the amounts shown.

| Instead of | Swap in | Change |
|---|---|---|
| The chicken in the batch curry (100 g raw a portion) | 40 g dried soya chunks a portion (160 g for the batch): the vegetarian curry below | About +35 kcal and −3 g protein a portion |
| Sardines on toast and an orange (the no-meat day's lunch) | Beans and cheese on toast and an orange: half a 415 g tin of baked beans, 2 slices of wholemeal toast, 30 g grated cheddar ("suitable for vegetarians") and 1 orange | About +15 kcal, −4.5 g protein and about 250 mg less calcium |

| Whey | Pea or soya protein powder | Much the same per scoop |
| Jelly sweets, gelatine desserts and capsules | Versions labelled vegan or "suitable for vegetarians" (or halal-certified) | None |

**Vegan?** Swap the milk and yoghurt for calcium-fortified soya drink and soya yoghurt; soya drink has about the same protein as semi-skimmed milk. [Chapter 14](14-nutrition.md) has the vitamin B12 note.

## Shopping on a budget

### Where to shop

- **Supermarket own brands** for milk, oats, yoghurt, tins and frozen vegetables. Use the unit price on the shelf label.
- **Asian grocers and big supermarkets' world-food aisles** for lentils, chana dal, soya chunks, spices and chapatis. Big bags are often far cheaper per kilo: 5 kg of red lentils was £9 when prices were checked.
- **Butchers,** including halal butchers, for chicken and mince. Ask about bulk boxes, then freeze in 100 g portions.

### Four label checks (if you eat halal or vegetarian)

1. **Gelatine.** Found in jelly sweets, marshmallows, some yoghurts and desserts, some protein bars and most capsules. It's usually from pork or beef unless the label says otherwise. Look for halal-certified, vegan or "suitable for vegetarians" versions.
2. **Rennet in cheese, and in whey.** Animal rennet comes from calves' stomachs. Cheese labelled "suitable for vegetarians" uses a non-animal rennet. Vegetarians avoid animal rennet; Muslims differ on whether it matters, so if you eat halal it's your call. Paneer is usually set with acid instead (check the label).
3. **Additives and flavourings.** The Halal Food Authority notes that food additives "can be animal-derived, synthetic, or plant-based". Two to know: E120 (cochineal or carmine, made from insects) and E471 (mono- and diglycerides, which can come from animal or plant fat). Some flavourings, such as vanilla extract, use alcohol as a carrier, and views on traces differ. A vegan or "suitable for vegetarians" label rules out animal sources, but not alcohol.
4. **Stock cubes, gravies and ready meals.** Meat-based ones are rarely halal-certified, and never vegetarian. Use vegetable stock cubes, or certified ones.

### If you eat halal: certification bodies

Several UK organisations certify halal food, and their standards differ:

- **Halal Monitoring Committee (HMC):** a registered charity. Its FAQ says "Part of our criteria is that animals are not stunned", and that its inspectors are "constantly present at the slaughterhouses during the slaughter process". HMC chicken carries a coloured, serialised label, and red meat an HMC stamp. Its website lists certified outlets.
- **Halal Food Authority (HFA):** a certification body that also certifies ingredients, additives and other products. Its FAQ says it allows "controlled electric stunning with minimal amperage, verified by a veterinary surgeon, for poultry and ovine meat", prohibits captive-bolt and percussion stunning, and doesn't accept mechanical slaughter.
- **Others** include mosque-approved butchers and international certifiers.

Muslims differ on stunned meat and on some foods, such as prawns and other shellfish. Choose the standard you're comfortable with, ask a butcher which body certifies them, and check the certificate is current.

## Batch cooking twice a week

The Food Standards Agency (FSA) says to eat leftovers within **two days** or freeze them. Each recipe makes 4 portions: eat 2 within two days and freeze 2. Two cooking sessions a week cover all seven days.

| | Sunday (about 90 minutes) | Wednesday (about 75 minutes) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cook** | Chicken or soya curry (4), soya keema (4), 6 boiled eggs, overnight oats if it's a no-meat week | Curry or chilli (4), soya keema or dal (4), 6 boiled eggs |
| **Eat fresh** | Monday and Tuesday | Thursday and Friday |
| **Freeze** | 2 of each, for Wednesday and Saturday | 2 of each, for Sunday and next Monday |

### Food-safety rules (FSA)

- Keep your fridge at **0–5°C**, and get cooked food into it within **1–2 hours**. Shallow tubs cool faster than one deep pot.
- **Eat leftovers within two days**, or freeze them. **Defrost in the fridge**, and eat within 24 hours of defrosting.
- Reheat until **steaming hot all the way through**, and only **once** after defrosting.
- **Rice:** chill it within an hour, eat it within 24 hours, and reheat it only once. If you're freezing rice, get it into the freezer within an hour of cooking.
- Treat overnight oats and boiled eggs the same way: eat them within two days. **[Expert opinion]**
- **Label every tub** with what's in it and the date, and freeze portions flat in bags so they stack and defrost overnight.

### Chicken, chickpea and spinach curry (4 portions)

**You need:** 400 g chicken breast or thigh (halal if you eat halal; ready-diced, or snipped with kitchen scissors), 2 tins chickpeas (drained), 2 tins chopped tomatoes, 300 g frozen diced onion, 320 g frozen spinach, 4 tbsp oil, 4 tsp garlic-ginger paste, 2 tsp each of cumin and garam masala, 1 tsp turmeric, and chilli powder and salt to taste.

1. Warm the oil in a large, heavy pan on a back hob. Cook the onion for 5–8 minutes.
2. Add the paste and spices for 1 minute, then the chicken, and stir for 5 minutes.
3. Add the tomatoes, chickpeas and a splash of water. Simmer for 20 minutes, until the chicken is cooked through with no pink.
4. Stir in the spinach from frozen for the last 5 minutes. Ladle it into four tubs rather than pouring from the pan.

Each portion is about **475 kcal and 40 g of protein** before rice, for roughly £1.60.

**Vegetarian version:** leave out the chicken and add 160 g dried soya mini chunks (or mince) with the tomatoes, plus about 350 ml more just-boiled water from the kettle, then simmer for 20 minutes. Each portion is about **510 kcal and 37 g of protein**, and it costs less.

### Soya keema with peas (4 portions)

**You need:** 100 g dried soya mince (or mini chunks), 300 g frozen diced onion, 2 tins chopped tomatoes, 320 g frozen peas, 4 tbsp oil, 4 tsp garlic-ginger paste, 2 tsp garam masala, 1 tsp cumin, chilli and salt to taste, and about 400 ml of just-boiled water from the kettle.

1. Cook the onion in the oil for 5–8 minutes, then the paste and spices for 1 minute.
2. Add the dry soya mince, the tomatoes and the water. Simmer for 15 minutes, stirring now and then, and add a splash more water if it gets dry. The soya soaks up the sauce, so there's nothing to drain or squeeze.
3. Add the peas for the last 5 minutes.

Each portion is about **340 kcal and 20 g of protein** before chapatis and raita, for roughly 70p. For the raita, stir a pinch of salt and ground cumin into 100 g of natural yoghurt.

### Red lentil and soya dal (4 portions)

**You need:** 200 g red lentils, 80 g dried soya mince, 300 g frozen diced onion, 2 tins chopped tomatoes, 320 g frozen spinach, 4 tbsp oil plus about 3 tbsp more for the tarka, 4 tsp garlic-ginger paste, 1 tsp turmeric, 2 tsp cumin (seeds for the tarka if you have them), chilli and salt to taste, and about 1 litre of water.

1. Cook the onion in 4 tbsp of oil for 5 minutes, then the paste and spices for 1 minute.
2. Add the lentils (rinsed in a sieve standing in the sink), the soya mince, the tomatoes and the water. Simmer for 20–25 minutes, stirring now and then, until thick.
3. Stir in the spinach from frozen for the last 5 minutes.
4. **Tarka (optional):** warm the remaining oil with the cumin seeds in a small pan for 30 seconds, then spoon it over. Leave the small pan on the hob, and never pour hot oil with one hand. Or simply stir the extra oil in at step 1.

Each portion is about **520 kcal and 29 g of protein** before rice, for roughly 85p.

### Mince, bean and lentil chilli (4 portions)

**You need:** 300 g beef or lamb mince (halal if you eat halal), 100 g red lentils, 1 tin kidney beans (drained), 2 tins chopped tomatoes, 300 g frozen diced onion, 2 tbsp oil, 2 tsp each of chilli powder, cumin and smoked paprika, and about 500 ml of water.

Cook it like the keema: onion first, then the mince, broken up with a wooden spoon until no pink remains, then the spices, lentils, beans, tomatoes and water. Simmer for 25 minutes. The lentils stretch 300 g of mince across four portions.

Each portion is about **430 kcal and 29 g of protein** before rice, for roughly £1.25 if the mince costs about £8 a kilo (not verified). **Vegetarian version:** use 100 g dried soya mince instead of the meat, added with the tomatoes and water, for about **360 kcal and 28 g of protein** a portion.

### Overnight oats and boiled eggs

- **Overnight oats:** per jar, stir 80 g oats, 250 ml milk, 150 g Greek-style yoghurt, 20 g peanut butter, 30 g raisins and 1 tsp honey in a tub with a lid, and leave it in the fridge overnight. Each is about **730 kcal and 33 g of protein**, for roughly 80p.
- **Boiled eggs:** cover 6 eggs with cold water, bring to the boil and simmer for 9 minutes. Lift them out with a slotted spoon into a bowl of cold water, and leave the pan on the hob to cool. Never carry or tip the pan.

> [!SAFETY]
> **Boiling water and hot oil are your biggest kitchen risks.** Never carry or tip a pan of boiling water or hot oil with one hand. Lift food out with a slotted spoon or spider strainer, and use a kettle for small amounts of hot water. Keep the injured arm well away from the hob and oven, and turn pan handles inwards.

## The one-handed kitchen

Until you're cleared, nothing should grip, hold, push or bear weight through the injured arm, and that includes cooking. The tips below are **[Expert opinion]**.

- **Set-up:** a non-slip mat or damp tea towel under boards and bowls; an electric or one-touch tin opener, or ring-pull tins; a jar-opener grip; kitchen scissors instead of a knife; a big microwave-safe jug for porridge; and a rice cooker or microwave rice steamer.
- **Pre-prepared foods:** frozen diced onion, frozen spinach and peas, garlic and ginger paste, ready-diced chicken, and shop-bought chapatis.
- **Falls are your biggest risk.** Wipe spills immediately and wear grippy shoes or slippers in the kitchen.
- **Use the back hobs**, and carry hot drinks in a mug with a lid.
- **Shopping follows the carrying rule** ([chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md) is its home). Until your arm reaches Light use / rehab started: nothing in your injured hand or on that shoulder. A light bag (3 kg or less) on your **uninjured** shoulder, or a waist pack, is fine; put it on and take it off with your uninjured hand. So get the big shop delivered, and keep top-up shops to what fits that bag. A day pack on both shoulders waits for that gate.

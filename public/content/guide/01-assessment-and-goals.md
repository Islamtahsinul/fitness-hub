# Your Assessment & Goals

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - Start with **your intake**: body, arm, clearances, training history, health, food, time and kit. The checklist is in [Start Here](00-start-here.md); this chapter explains what each answer changes.
> - **Your numbers come from formulas.** For the example profile (a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult): about **3,060 kcal** (male) or **2,800 kcal** (female) and **144 g of protein** a day, **HRmax about 187** and **Zone 2 at about 112–131 bpm**. The hub works out yours.
> - Your first week: the PAR-Q+, your hub profile (add your usual pre-injury weight if you know it), the baseline tests on days 1–3, and an exact log of your Re-entry Week leg-press calibration set, which is your strength baseline.
> - The goal templates use the hub's weight bands (**+0.25–0.5 kg a week** while you regain to your pre-injury weight, then **+0.15–0.3**) and aim for about **+30% on the leg press** by the end of Phase 1. Lost muscle comes back over months; new muscle beyond your old peak comes more slowly.
> - Your "minimum standards" are the few things you do even in your worst week.

## How a coach onboards you

If you've trained for years, nobody needs to teach you to lift. You still get the onboarding any client gets when they come back from an injury: **screen** that training is safe for you now, **interview** you about your history and life, **test** a baseline, **set goals**, then **plan and review**. Steps 1 to 4 happen in your first week. Step 5 runs for as long as you use the programme: a readiness check every morning, a weekly review, retests every 4 weeks and a goals review every 12 weeks.

Andy Galpin's approach is a good model: assessment comes before any protocol, and he lists nine qualities training can improve, from skill and strength to hypertrophy and long-duration endurance. **[Expert opinion]** The programme's five goals map onto them, so we test the qualities that matter for those goals.

| Your goal | Main qualities involved | What we measure |
|---|---|---|
| Functional fitness | Strength, skill, muscular endurance | Your functional "decathlon", sit-to-stand, balance |
| All-terrain long-distance hiking | Long-duration endurance, leg strength | Zone 2 minutes, resting HR, the walk and stair tests |
| Core stability | Skill, strength, muscular endurance | Control and hold times now; carries later |
| Farmer strength | Strength, grip, muscular endurance | Leg and hip strength now; carries and grip once cleared |
| +5 kg of muscle | Hypertrophy | Weight trend, waist and thigh measurements, rep strength |

## Your numbers: the formulas, worked through

The hub calculates these from your profile, and they update as your weight changes. The right-hand column works them through for an **example profile: a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult**. It isn't a target body; it just shows the arithmetic. Your own numbers appear on **Nutrition** (calories and protein) and **Settings** (heart-rate zones) once your profile is filled in.

| What | How it's worked out | Example profile |
|---|---|---|
| BMI | Weight (kg) ÷ height (m)². A rough check only: the NHS notes BMI "cannot tell the difference between muscle and fat" | 80 ÷ 1.8² = **24.7** |
| Waist target | The NHS advises keeping your waist under half your height | Under **90 cm** |
| BMR | Mifflin–St Jeor: 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age, then **+5 for men** or **−161 for women** | **1,780 kcal** (male) or **1,614 kcal** (female) |
| Maintenance | BMR × 1.55, the hub's default activity factor (range 1.375–1.725) | About **2,760 kcal** (male) or **2,500 kcal** (female) |
| Daily target | Maintenance + 300 kcal: a small surplus, never a diet while the bone heals | About **3,060 kcal** (male) or **2,800 kcal** (female) |
| Protein | 1.8 g/kg (range 1.6–2.2 g/kg) | **144 g** (range 128–176 g) |
| Weight band | **+0.25–0.5 kg a week** below your pre-injury weight, **+0.15–0.3** at or above it, **+0.2–0.4** if it isn't set. The hub's bands, judged once a fortnight ([chapter 14](14-nutrition.md)) | +0.2–0.4 kg a week with no pre-injury weight set |
| HRmax | Tanaka: 208 − 0.7 × age | 208 − 0.7 × 30 = **187 bpm** |
| Zone 2 | 60–70% of HRmax. Check: you can talk in full sentences but would rather not, and you can breathe through your nose | **112–131 bpm** |
| Other zones | 50–60%, 70–80%, 80–90% and 90–100% of HRmax | Z1 94–112, Z3 131–150, Z4 150–168, Z5 168–187 bpm |
| MAF ceiling | MAF 180 − age, then −10 while you're recovering from injury (Maffetone's −10 covers recovery from an operation and time in rehab), as on the hub's Settings page. A ceiling for easy days, not a target ([chapter 10](10-engine-cardio.md)) | 180 − 30 = **150**, then −10 = **140 bpm** |
| Steps | The programme's step targets | 8,000 a day by week 4, building to 10,000 |

## Step 1 — Health screening

### The PAR-Q+

The PAR-Q+ (the Physical Activity Readiness Questionnaire for Everyone) is the standard pre-exercise screening form, and an injury like yours is exactly what it's for. Download the current 2025 version from [eparmedx.com](https://eparmedx.com/) ([direct PDF](https://eparmedx.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/PARQPlus2025Fillable.pdf)).

**You'll answer YES to the bone, joint or soft-tissue question.** That's expected. A "yes" sends you to the follow-up pages, which may point you to its online tool (the ePARmed-X+), or advise you to speak to a qualified exercise professional or your doctor. Do exactly what it says. The form is valid for 12 months, or until your health changes, and it advises delaying any increase in activity during a temporary illness such as a cold or fever.

### When to see your GP first

The NHS says: "Speak to your GP first if you have not exercised for some time, or if you have medical conditions or concerns." If you have no other conditions, you've kept walking through the layoff, and you're returning gradually to training you know well, that's a low-risk way back.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Any PAR-Q+ "yes" apart from the fracture | See your GP before you raise the intensity |
| You start a medicine that changes heart rate | Ask your GP or pharmacist whether your zones still apply, and use the talk test until you know **[Expert opinion]** |
| Chest pain or pressure, fainting, or severe breathlessness during exercise | Stop. This is an emergency: call 999 |
| Any arm red flag, or arm pain of 7/10 or more ([chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md)) | RED day: no training at all, physio exercises included, until you've been advised. Contact your fracture clinic today, or NHS 111 / 999 as the red-flag guidance says |
| Dark (cola-coloured) urine with severe muscle pain or swelling after training | Get same-day medical advice (possible rhabdomyolysis): your GP or NHS 111, today |
| Questions about what your arm can do | Your fracture clinic or physio, not your GP and not this guide |

### Your arm: who decides

Your fracture clinic and physio decide what your injured arm can do. This guide never overrides them. At your next fracture-clinic appointment, take the clinic questions from [chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md), ask for any restrictions in writing, and ask about a physio referral if you don't have one yet.

After every appointment, enter any new clearance date on **Your arm**. The hub uses those dates to unlock the gates: **Good arm OK**, **Light use / rehab started**, **Grip cleared** and **Full clearance**.

> [!SAFETY]
> Until the relevant gate is cleared, nothing grips, holds, pushes, pulls, braces or bears weight through your injured arm, and nothing risks a fall. Testing follows the same rules. No test is worth a setback.

## Step 2 — Baseline testing

### Re-entry Week (programme week 1)

Your tests live on **Progress → Tests**. Each has a gate: the hub only offers the ones you're cleared for, and shows each one as due only from its scheduled time. [Chapter 04](04-the-program.md) owns the timing; before any clearance it works out like this:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Every morning, from the day before your programme starts | Your check-in: your weight on the scale and a 60-second resting pulse before you get up ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)). The hub's 7-day average is your weight baseline |
| Days 1–3 | The baseline tests on **Progress → Tests**, after your normal warm-up: **Monday:** 30-second sit-to-stand, knee-to-wall ankle test and eyes-open single-leg balance. **Tuesday:** the 1-mile brisk walk, on the same treadmill and settings you'll use for retests. **Wednesday:** single-leg heel raise and hollow hold. Log your waist and thigh on **Progress** on day 1 |
| Your Re-entry Week sessions | **Your leg-press baseline:** your best leg-press calibration set of the week, the one at RIR 2 after your two easy sets. Log the plates and reps exactly. The hub compares estimated 1RMs (Epley), so a set of 8 or 13 counts as well as a set of 10 |

Grip in your uninjured hand joins once Good arm OK is set, and lives on **Your arm**. Your injured hand is measured only as your physio directs.

### Rules for every test day

- **GREEN days only.** If your check-in says AMBER or RED, move the test.
- **Same conditions every time:** morning, a similar breakfast and caffeine, the same warm-up, machine, settings and shoes. Note seat positions and machine levels.
- **Same standard every rep.** A rep only counts if it meets the test's standard.
- **No maximal breath-holding (Valsalva).** Brace moderately and breathe out on the effort.
- **Balance tests next to a support** your uninjured hand can touch.
- **Stop** if your arm hurts or a red flag appears.

### Retesting every 4 weeks

The hub reminds you when retests are due. Use the same conditions as your baseline, compare you with you, and look at trends across 8–12 weeks, because single results are noisy. A test is not a workout, so don't add hard sets afterwards.

**Why the engine and strength matter long term:** in about 122,000 adults who had treadmill tests, fitness was linked to lower long-term mortality, with no upper limit of benefit seen. In about 140,000 adults across 17 countries, each 5 kg of lower grip strength was linked to a 16% higher risk of death. **[Moderate evidence]** Both show association, not cause. Grip is a marker of overall strength, not a magic lever.

## Step 3 — Body composition and your muscle timeline

You're regaining the muscle you've lost since the injury, then adding more, accepting a little fat along the way, and giving your bone everything it needs to heal. That means **no dieting** while the bone heals.

- **Scale weight:** every morning, judged on the hub's 7-day average ([chapter 14](14-nutrition.md)). If it's a smart scale, ignore its body-fat figure ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)).
- **Waist:** measure around your middle, halfway between your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone, after a normal breath out, at the same spot every time (NHS). Keep it under half your height.
- **Tape measurements:** thigh, calf, chest, hips and upper arm, with good and injured sides recorded separately. Measure the injured forearm only once the wound has fully healed and it's comfortable, and never over a dressing or the scar.
- **Photos (optional):** private, with the same light, clothes and poses every 4 weeks.

### Regain first, then new muscle

Muscle and strength you've had before tend to come back faster than they were first built. **[Moderate evidence]** Women who had stopped training for 30–32 weeks kept part of their strength through the break, and their fast muscle fibres grew significantly within 6 weeks of retraining. In a study of 8 men, 7 weeks of retraining after a 7-week break took leg lean mass to 12.4% above baseline, against 6.5% after the first 7 weeks of training. Not every study agrees: one found that a previously trained leg regrew no faster than an untrained one after 20 weeks off, and a 2020 review calls the mechanism in humans "largely ambiguous". So treat muscle memory as a real advantage, not a guarantee. Strength returns fastest of all, because the skill of each lift never left.

| What | When, if the gates arrive at a sensible pace | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Strength in your leg lifts | Re-entry Week to the end of Phase 1 (about programme week 9) | Fast. About +30% on the leg press from a cautious re-entry baseline is realistic |
| Leg and trunk muscle you've lost | Roughly the first 3–4 months of training, plus any fasting pause | Comes back as you gain in the regain band |
| Upper-body muscle you've lost | From Good arm OK (your uninjured arm) and Grip cleared (both arms) | A few months of regain, counted from whenever each gate opens |
| New muscle beyond your old peak | Once you've regained, so mostly in the second half of your first year | Slow after years of training: months per kilogram, not weeks |

These timings are **[Expert opinion]**: no study has followed an experienced lifter back from a forearm fracture. [Chapter 08](08-building-muscle.md) has the full gain model, and [chapter 03](03-training-principles.md) the science of coming back after a layoff.

**What "+5 kg of muscle" means for you.** Part of it is the muscle you've lost since the injury, and that part comes back over months. The rest is new muscle beyond your previous peak, and that comes slowly. Your pre-injury weight and measurements are the best clue to how big the first part is, which is one more reason to add your usual weight in **Settings**.

## Step 4 — Setting your goals

### Outcome, performance and process goals

Your goals mix all three: outcomes ("back to my pre-injury weight by programme week 12"), performance ("leg press up 30% on my re-entry baseline") and process ("my protein target on 6 days out of 7"). **Process goals come first.** You don't control gate dates, so some outcome dates are uncertain, but you control 100% of your process goals.

**What the evidence says.** A meta-analysis of randomised trials found a small, reliable positive effect of goal setting on behaviour (d = 0.34), and goals set publicly worked particularly well. **[Strong evidence]** SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) is a useful checklist, though a 2023 critique argues it lacks a clear scientific basis. **[Expert opinion]** So we use it as a checklist and pair it with process goals.

### Your 12-week goals (Re-entry Week and Phase 1)

These are templates. Copy them into your notes and put your own numbers in; the examples use the example profile. Week 12 ends on the Sunday of programme week 12. If a fasting period falls inside those weeks, judge the weight goal on your 7-day average from the last week before the fast: the hub gives no weight verdict during a fasting period or for the 14 days after it.

| Area | SMART goal template | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | Gain at your band's rate, judged by the hub's fortnightly verdict: **+0.25–0.5 kg a week** while your 7-day average is below your pre-injury weight, then **+0.15–0.3**, or **+0.2–0.4** if you haven't set one. Over 12 weeks that means back at your pre-injury weight if it's within about 4 kg of today's. For the 80 kg example with no pre-injury weight set, it's a 7-day average of roughly **82–85 kg** ([chapter 14](14-nutrition.md) owns the bands) | Outcome |
| Waist | Rising no faster than about 1 cm a month, and under half your height (90 cm for the 180 cm example) | Outcome |
| Protein | **Your protein target or more on at least 6 days out of 7** (1.8 g/kg: 144 g for the 80 kg example), logged in the check-in, every week | Process |
| Training | **Adherence of 80% or more** each month (sessions done ÷ sessions planned, as your **Weekly review** shows; RED and fasting days don't count against you), using **Short on time?** on bad days rather than skipping | Process |
| Leg strength | Leg press: your best estimated 1RM about **30% above** your Re-entry Week calibration set by the end of Phase 1 week 8 (programme week 9), and a higher 30-second sit-to-stand at every retest | Performance |
| Engine | **150 or more Zone 2 minutes a week from Phase 1 week 3** (deload weeks are planned lower and don't count), and a faster 1-mile walk at the same or a lower finishing heart rate | Process and performance |
| Resting HR | 7-day average **at least 3 bpm below** your baseline by week 12, measured the same way ([chapter 17](17-sleep-and-recovery.md)) | Performance **[Expert opinion]** |
| Steps | Average **8,000 a day by week 4**, and 9,000–10,000 by week 12 | Process |
| Core and balance | Dead bug (legs only) 3 × 8 per side with no back arching; tucked hollow hold 3 × 30 s; single-leg balance 30 s per side with head turns, next to a support | Performance |
| Hiking | Your Saturday long walk at the programme's full **80 minutes** on good paths, with no aches the next day, at least twice. Longer walks are optional extras on free weekends, within the readiness rules; [chapter 13](13-uk-hiking-roadmap.md) has route ideas | Performance |
| Recovery | 7.5 hours or more in bed on at least 6 nights a week | Process |

**Why +30% on the leg press.** Your calibration set comes after a long layoff, at RIR 2, on a machine you're setting up one-handed, so it will sit well below your old best. Strength you've had before comes back quickly: in one study, older men needed less than 8 weeks of retraining to regain their trained 1RM after a 12-week break. **[Moderate evidence]** The exact figure is **[Expert opinion]**. If you pass it early, good: keep to the plan's increments rather than chasing your old numbers.

All numbers are starting targets **[Expert opinion]**, and you rewrite them at each 12-week review.

### Your 6- and 12-month goals

These assume the gates arrive at a sensible pace. If a gate comes later, the matching outcomes slide back by the same amount, and the process goals carry on unchanged.

| Area | By programme month 6 | By programme month 12 |
|---|---|---|
| Muscle and weight | Leg and trunk muscle regained, and upper-body regain under way where the gates allow. At or above your pre-injury weight (or, with none set, a trend that has stayed in your band at most fortnightly verdicts), waist still under half your height | Lost muscle regained wherever the gates have allowed, and new muscle beyond your old peak started. Weight still rising slowly in the lean-gain band, waist under half your height |
| Strength | Training the whole body on machines and dumbbells, if Grip cleared, and closing in on your pre-injury numbers on the leg lifts | Steady progress on every main lift towards and past your pre-injury bests, with no 1RM testing |
| Farmer strength | If Grip cleared: the "Starting out" standard in [chapter 09](09-farmer-strength.md), 4 × 30 s at about 10% of body weight per hand (8 kg for the 80 kg example), building towards 15% (12 kg) as the grip ladder allows | Chapter 09's "Solid" standard, at Full clearance with grip at 90% or more: a quarter of your body weight per hand (20 kg each for the example) for 60 s. Half your body weight per hand ("Strong") is the long-term goal |
| Core | Suitcase carries and Pallof presses, from Grip cleared | Plank 60 s, side plank 30 s per side and bird dog with good form, at Full clearance |
| Engine | 90 minutes of continuous Zone 2 | Your aerobic threshold found with the drift test ([chapter 10](10-engine-cardio.md)) |
| Hiking | Mission 1 in [chapter 13](13-uk-hiking-roadmap.md), in the version your gate and phase allow | Mission 2 in chapter 13, a big mountain or challenge day, only once your gate and phase allow it (chapter 13 says when); otherwise its fallback |

> [!SAFETY]
> If your first 12 weeks fall in a UK winter, a "good path" can be icy, wet or muddy, and a fall onto the healing arm is the biggest risk you have. On those days, do the long session on the treadmill or stair climber instead.

### Your functional "decathlon"

Peter Attia lists the physical tasks he wants to do late in life, then works backwards to what he must train now ("backcasting"). **[Expert opinion]** "Very functional", the first goal, gets defined the same way: tasks you can test. Edit this draft and keep your version in your notes.

| Task | When you can work on it |
|---|---|
| Walk 25 km across mixed terrain in a day, with a pack | Engine and legs now. Rough ground and loaded rucking at Full clearance |
| Carry two heavy shopping bags up three flights of stairs | Legs and trunk now. Carrying at Grip cleared |
| Carry a 25 kg sandbag 50 m | Full clearance |
| Get up off the floor without using your hands | Low box squats and step-downs build it now. Floor get-ups only once your physio is happy |
| Lift a 10 kg case into an overhead luggage rack | Full clearance |
| Stand on one leg for 30 seconds while turning your head, next to a support | Now |
| Walk down a steep, rough descent with control | Step-downs and Patrick steps now. Rough ground at Full clearance |
| Hang from a bar for 60 seconds | Full clearance |
| Climb 1,000 m of ascent in a day | Stair climber and incline walking now |
| Sprint 20 m for a bus | Full clearance |

### Minimum standards: your non-negotiables

These are the floor, not the target. You hit them even in a terrible week.

1. **Safety first.** Never train past a gate. A red flag means stop and call.
2. **Readiness check every morning.** It takes about a minute.
3. **Move every day.** At least the 10-minute mobility routine and a 20-minute walk, even on rest days. RED days are the exception: then you call the clinic.
4. **Minimum session on bad days**, and never miss twice ([chapter 20](20-habits-and-mindset.md)).
5. **Eat for healing.** No dieting. Hit your protein target most days.
6. **Sleep window of at least 7.5 hours.**
7. **Log everything** in the hub.
8. **Weekly review:** 10 minutes every Sunday.
9. **Physio homework as prescribed**, from the day it starts.

## How the gates shape your timeline

| Gate | What it opens (examples) | Goals it moves forward |
|---|---|---|
| **Now** | Legs on machines and bodyweight, the engine, core work without arm load, balance next to a support, mobility, walks on good surfaces | Hiking engine, core control, leg muscle |
| **Good arm OK** | The seated good-arm add-on: one-arm machine and dumbbell work for your uninjured arm, three days a week, with no carries | Upper-body muscle (one side), and cross-education for your injured arm |
| **Light use / rehab started** | The physio rehab add-on (once your physio's exercises are entered on **Your arm**), the couch stretch, and a light day pack on walks, starting at 2–4 kg ([chapter 18](18-arm-recovery.md) has the carrying rule) | Arm recovery, walking with a light pack |
| **Grip cleared** | Gripping and holding light-to-moderate weights, two-handed machines, carries by the grip ladder, cable core work, trekking poles | Farmer strength, full upper body, core |
| **Full clearance** | Heavy loading, hanging, push-ups and planks, impact and jumping, rough terrain | Everything, including mountain terrain |

Gates are cumulative: clearing a higher gate counts for all the gates below it, so Light use / rehab started also opens everything under Good arm OK. If a gate is late, the hub keeps you in your current phase and repeats its later weeks. That isn't failure: it's more time building the legs and engine that hiking needs most.

### Three ways your next appointment could go

- **You're cleared to train your uninjured arm and/or referred to physio.** Keep the 12-week goals and add the new work.
- **Nothing changes yet.** Keep the Phase 1 goals, push the upper-body and carry outcomes back by however long the gate takes, and ask when the next review and the physio referral will be.
- **A setback** (for example, more surgery or slow healing). Reset your goals around the clinic's timeline. Your engine, leg and habit goals still stand.

# Building Muscle

> [!NOTE]
> **In short**
> - Your 5 kg comes at two speeds. First you **regain** what the layoff took: muscle you've had before usually comes back faster than it took to build. Then you **add new muscle** beyond your previous best, which after years of training is slow.
> - **Legs first.** They're your biggest muscles, you can train them hard right now without the arm, and they power every hike. Your upper body regains as each clearance arrives.
> - The recipe: about **10–14 hard sets a week** for quads and glutes and 8–10 for hamstrings and calves in Phase 1, mostly **6–20 reps**, stopping **1–3 reps short of failure**, adding reps and then load.
> - **Eat for it:** maintenance plus about **300 kcal a day** and about **1.8 g of protein per kg** of body weight (about 145 g for the 80 kg example profile). No dieting while the bone heals.
> - **Your weight band:** +0.25 to +0.5 kg a week while you're below your usual pre-injury weight, then +0.15 to +0.3. Without a pre-injury weight in Settings, +0.2 to +0.4. The hub gives one verdict a fortnight.

You want about 5 kg of muscle: more strength for carrying, more robust knees and hips on long descents, and a body that ages better. You've built muscle before, so you know the recipe. This chapter is the honest version of how it works on the way back from a layoff, with one arm out of action for months.

**Your starting numbers** come from your profile, and the hub recalculates them as your weight changes (the calculator is on the **Nutrition** page). Here's how they're worked out, with the **example profile**: a 30-year-old, 80 kg, 180 cm adult.

| | How it's worked out | Example profile |
|---|---|---|
| Resting energy (Mifflin–St Jeor) | 10 × kg + 6.25 × cm − 5 × age, then +5 (men) or −161 (women) | about 1,780 kcal a day (men) or 1,610 (women) |
| Maintenance | Resting energy × activity factor (1.55 by default) | about 2,760 or 2,500 kcal a day |
| Target | Maintenance + 300 (hub default) | about 3,060 or 2,800 kcal a day; more on long-walk and hike days |
| Protein | 1.8 g per kg of body weight (hub default) | about 145 g a day, roughly 4 × 36 g |
| Weight band | Regain +0.25 to +0.5 kg a week below your usual pre-injury weight; lean gain +0.15 to +0.3 after that; +0.2 to +0.4 if it isn't set | — |
| Your usual weight before the injury | Add it in Settings if you know it (optional): it decides which band applies | — |

## What "5 kg of muscle" really means

Muscle gain and scale gain are not the same thing.

- **Muscle** is the tissue you build with training, protein and enough food.
- **The scale** also moves with water, the stored carbohydrate in your muscles (glycogen), food in your gut and a little body fat. When you start training and eating more after a layoff, the scale often jumps in the first week or two from glycogen and water alone **[Expert opinion]**.

So your weight will rise a little more than your muscle does. That's normal and fine. What you're watching for is weight rising **inside your band**, your **waist staying roughly stable**, your **thighs** (and later chest and arms) **growing**, and your **lifts climbing**. That combination means most of the gain is muscle.

## Two speeds: regain, then new muscle

### Regain: muscle memory

A layoff of a few months costs some muscle and more strength, and both usually come back faster than they were first built. The research behind that, and the debate about why (extra nuclei in the muscle fibres, or an "epigenetic" memory in the muscle's DNA), is in [Training Principles](03-training-principles.md#11-coming-back-after-a-layoff-muscle-memory). The practical points:
- **Strength returns first**, within weeks, because much of it is skill your nervous system has kept **[Moderate evidence]**.
- **Size follows** over the first few months of consistent training and eating. How fast varies from person to person **[Expert opinion]**.
- **Your legs start now; your upper body starts when it's cleared.** Chest, back and arms begin their regain in Phase 2a at the earliest.

### New muscle: slower

Once you're back to your previous best, every extra kilogram is new muscle, and for someone with years of training that comes slowly. A review of off-season bodybuilding nutrition suggests gaining about **0.25–0.5% of body weight a week**, and being more conservative the more advanced you are ([Iraki et al., 2019](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22nutrition%20recommendations%20for%20bodybuilders%20in%20the%20off-season%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Expert opinion]**. For the 80 kg example profile that's about 0.2–0.4 kg a week, and your lean-gain band (+0.15 to +0.3 kg) sits at the conservative end of it. Gaining faster mostly adds fat: elite athletes whose food was planned for a bigger surplus gained more weight (3.9% versus 1.5% of body weight) and much more fat, but no more lean mass ([Garthe et al., 2013](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=AUTH:%22Garthe%20I%22%20AND%20TITLE:%22body%20composition%22&format=json&resultType=core&pageSize=6)) **[Moderate evidence]**.

### Your gain model

The hub picks your band from your 7-day average weight and your usual weight before the injury (Settings, optional):

| Situation | Weekly band | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Below your usual pre-injury weight | **+0.25 to +0.5 kg** | Regain: muscle memory brings lost muscle back faster than new muscle is built |
| At or above it | **+0.15 to +0.3 kg** | Lean gain: new muscle beyond your previous best is slow, so a smaller surplus keeps fat down |
| Pre-injury weight not set | **+0.2 to +0.4 kg** | A middle band until you add it |

How the hub judges it:
- **One verdict a fortnight**, on non-overlapping 14-day windows with at least 10 weigh-ins. The goals card and the coach notes show the same verdict.
- **Fasting windows and the 14 days after them don't count**, so a fasting period never triggers a "gain more" nudge.
- **Below the band** for a whole fortnight: [Nutrition](14-nutrition.md) explains the food change. **Above it:** usually ease back a little. Chapter 14 owns the food rules; this chapter owns the training.

**Example.** Say you weigh 80 kg now (the example profile) and your usual weight was 82.5 kg: the regain band applies until your 7-day average reaches 82.5 kg, about 5–10 weeks at +0.25–0.5 kg a week. After that the lean-gain band takes over.

### How long to +5 kg?

| Scenario | Regain | New muscle | Rough time to about +5 kg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gates on time, food and sleep in place | Legs from the start, upper body from Phase 2a (programme week 10 at the earliest) | Slow and steady once you pass your old best | about 12–18 months |
| Late clearances or a patchy year | Legs on time, upper body later | Slower | 18–24 months or more |

These figures are **[Expert opinion]**, and they depend on how much of the 5 kg is regain. Your pre-injury weight answers that: the closer you are to it now, the more of the 5 kg is new muscle and the longer it takes. For the first months most of your gain will come from your legs and hips, which hold a large share of your muscle anyway.

## The legs-first strategy

Your lower body holds your biggest muscles: quads, glutes, hamstrings, adductors and calves. With the right machines you can train them **hard and progressively right now**, with no grip and no involvement of your injured arm.

**Why it's the right call:**
1. **It's where the muscle is.** Big muscles have the most room to grow, so they offer the most kilograms for your effort **[Expert opinion]**.
2. **You can overload them safely now.** The dead-start leg press, single-leg press, leg curl and leg extension don't need hands, and bodyweight single-leg work (rear-foot-elevated split squats, single-leg hip thrusts) gets hard with a slow lowering and a pause.
3. **It's exactly what hiking needs.** Climbing power, controlled descents and knee resilience.
4. **The upper body isn't idle.** Once you're cleared, the good-arm block trains your uninjured side, and may help your injured arm hold on to strength ([Your Arm](18-arm-recovery.md)).

**Phase 1 lower-body volume** (hard sets a week, full sets from week 1; at most six exercises a session):

| Muscle | Main exercises | Hard sets/week |
|---|---|---|
| Quads | Dead-start leg press, rear-foot-elevated split squat (Mon); single-leg press, leg extension (Fri) | about 12 |
| Glutes | Rear-foot-elevated split squat (Mon); single-leg hip thrust, single-leg RDL, side-lying abduction (Wed); adductor-squeeze bridge (Fri); both presses (half) | about 13 |
| Hamstrings | Hamstring curl (Wed and Fri); single-leg RDL and single-leg hip thrust (half) | about 9 |
| Calves | Single-leg calf raise (Mon), single-leg bent-knee calf raise (Wed) | about 8 |

The adductors get direct work from the adductor-squeeze bridge on Fridays and indirect work from the presses and split squats. The Friday circuit, Patrick steps and tibialis raises are extra and not counted.

## The levers that build muscle

| Lever | Your target | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly volume** | About 10–14 hard sets per muscle in Phase 1, spread over 2–3 sessions; more for quads and glutes from Phase 2 | More sets, more growth, in a graded way ([Schoenfeld et al., 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22dose-response%20relationship%20between%20weekly%20resistance%20training%20volume%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Moderate evidence]**; about 10 sets/week as a minimum, 15–20 often more productive ([Galpin on Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/episode/dr-andy-galpin-how-to-build-strength-muscle-size-and-endurance)) **[Expert opinion]** |
| **Effort** | Stop 1–3 reps short of failure | No clear advantage for failure ([Refalo et al., 2023](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Refalo%20proximity%20to%20failure%20hypertrophy%20meta-analysis&format=json&resultType=core)); 1–2 RIR grew muscle as well as failure over 8 weeks ([Refalo et al., 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=%28TITLE:%22repetitions%20in%20reserve%22%20AND%20%28AUTH:%22Zourdos%22%20OR%20AUTH:%22Helms%22%29%29&format=json&resultType=core&pageSize=8)) **[Moderate evidence]** |
| **Reps and load** | Mostly 6–20 reps: lower on big lifts, higher on machines and bodyweight single-leg work | Light and heavy loads grow muscle similarly when sets are hard; heavy loads build more maximal strength ([Schoenfeld et al., 2017](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22how%20many%20times%20per%20week%20should%20a%20muscle%20be%20trained%22%20OR%20TITLE:%22strength%20and%20hypertrophy%20adaptations%20between%20low-%20vs.%20high-load%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Moderate evidence]**; roughly 5–30 reps all work near failure (Galpin) **[Expert opinion]** |
| **Frequency** | Each muscle twice a week or more | Frequency matters little when weekly volume is equal ([Schoenfeld et al., 2019](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22how%20many%20times%20per%20week%20should%20a%20muscle%20be%20trained%22%20OR%20TITLE:%22strength%20and%20hypertrophy%20adaptations%20between%20low-%20vs.%20high-load%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Moderate evidence]**; splitting volume keeps sessions short and sets high quality |
| **Rest between sets** | 2–3 min on big lifts, 60–90 s on machines and small muscles | ACSM suggests about 1–2 min for hypertrophy and longer for heavy strength work ([ACSM, 2009](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22progression%20models%20in%20resistance%20training%20for%20healthy%20adults%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Moderate evidence]** |
| **Progressive overload** | Double progression, every week you can | The core rule of training ([Training Principles](03-training-principles.md)) |
| **Full range, both lengths** | Train muscles at long lengths (rear-foot-elevated split squats, RDLs) and short ones (leg extension, hip-thrust squeeze) | Huberman's protocol pairs a "shortened" and a "lengthened" exercise per muscle ([Huberman Lab](https://www.hubermanlab.com/newsletter/foundational-fitness-protocol)) **[Expert opinion]** |
| **Consistency** | Six mornings a week, month after month | The multiplier on everything else **[Expert opinion]** |

### How close to failure, on the way back

**RIR 1–3 is the sweet spot.** An exploratory analysis suggests muscle growth creeps up as sets get closer to failure, while strength gains look similar across a wide range ([Robinson et al., 2024](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=Refalo%20proximity%20to%20failure%20hypertrophy%20meta-analysis&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Moderate evidence]**. Where you sit in that range depends on the exercise, not on how experienced you are:
- **Machine leg work** (leg press, leg extension, hamstring curl): RIR 1–2 from Phase 1 week 4. These are stable and easy to stop, and the leg press's stops catch the sled.
- **The single-leg press:** RIR 2–3. One leg can fail suddenly.
- **Free-standing and balance-demanding work** (split squats, step-ups, single-leg RDLs): RIR 2–3. A wobble near failure is a fall risk, and a fall is the one thing you must avoid before full clearance.
- **Hip thrusts and calf raises:** RIR 1–2 from Phase 3, once they're loaded and stable.
- **Upper-body work in Phases 2a–3:** RIR 2–3 while the forearm is still adapting; RIR 1–2 only from Phase 4, after full clearance.
- **Never to failure**, and never with a breath-hold before clearance.

## Your weekly sets, phase by phase

| Phase | Lower body (hard sets/week) | Upper body (hard sets/week) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Re-entry Week | 3 sets of each main lift, the last one a calibration set at RIR 2 | 0 | Finding your loads |
| 1 Foundation | Quads about 12, glutes about 13, hamstrings about 9, calves about 8 | 0 (15 sets for your uninjured arm in each add-on session once Good arm OK is set) | Regain phase for the legs; full sets from week 1 |
| 2a Machines First | Quads about 12, glutes about 16, hamstrings about 8, calves about 2 | Chest 3, back 10, shoulders 4, arms 6 | Two weeks to get both hands used to load |
| 2 Rebuild | Quads about 12, glutes about 15, hamstrings about 8, calves about 3 | Chest 6, back 10, shoulders 7, arms 9 | Free weights come back light |
| 3 Build | Quads about 13, glutes about 16, hamstrings about 8, calves about 3 | Chest 6, back 9, shoulders 6, arms 13 | Main whole-body hypertrophy block, upper body at RIR 2–3 |
| 4a Bridge | Quads about 12, glutes about 14, hamstrings about 7, calves about 2 | Chest 6, back 9, shoulders 3, arms 8 | New full-clearance skills first; sets capped in weeks 1–3 |
| 4 Forge | Quads about 11, glutes about 10, hamstrings about 4, calves about 2 | Chest 6, back 6, shoulders 6, arms 8 | More strength and hiking; size still progressing |
| 5 Mission | Quads about 10, glutes about 10, hamstrings about 4, calves about 2 | Chest 3, back 6, shoulders 5, arms 6 | Maintenance plus mission prep |

Counting method, as in [Training Principles](03-training-principles.md): a set counts fully for its main movers and half for helpers, and circuits, holds, carries, swings, jumps and the knee and shin armour aren't counted. From Phase 2 your calves get their volume from stair climbing, pack walks and hikes, and in Phases 4–5 deadlifts, swings and hills do much of the hinge work. If you've gained well and want more upper-body size in Phases 4–5, add one set to each upper-body exercise rather than adding new exercises, and keep sessions inside 90 minutes **[Expert opinion]**.

## Cardio and muscle can coexist

You'll do a lot of walking and climbing. Good news: across 43 studies, combining endurance and strength training didn't compromise muscle growth or maximal strength. Only explosive power was slightly blunted, mostly when both were done in the same session ([Schumann et al., 2022](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22compatibility%20of%20concurrent%20aerobic%20and%20strength%20training%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Strong evidence]**. Two practical points:
- Keep most cardio genuinely easy (Zone 2). Your hard intervals are on non-lifting days.
- **Long walks burn a lot of energy.** On long-walk and hike days, eat more. Otherwise the surplus you need for muscle quietly disappears.

## Eating to grow (the short version)

Nutrition gets its own chapters: [Nutrition](14-nutrition.md), [Meal Plans](15-meal-plans.md), [Supplements](16-supplements.md) and [Sleep & Recovery](17-sleep-and-recovery.md). The essentials for muscle are:

- **A small surplus:** about **+250–300 kcal a day** above maintenance; for the example profile that's about 3,060 kcal on a normal day for a man or 2,800 for a woman. The hub works out your maintenance from the Mifflin–St Jeor equation and your activity level. **No dieting while the bone heals.** Bone and muscle both need the raw materials. Budget protein ideas (with halal-friendly and vegetarian swaps) and full meal plans are in [Meal Plans](15-meal-plans.md).
- **Protein: 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight a day**, spread over 3–4 meals. In a large meta-analysis, protein beyond about 1.6 g/kg/day gave no further average gain in fat-free mass ([Morton et al., 2018](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=TITLE:%22systematic%20review%2C%20meta-analysis%20and%20meta-regression%20of%20the%20effect%20of%20protein%20supplementation%22&format=json&resultType=core)) **[Strong evidence]**. The range above that covers individual differences and the bone's needs.

| Body weight | Range (1.6–2.2 g/kg) | Hub default (1.8 g/kg) | Per meal (4 meals) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70 kg | 112–154 g | about 125 g | about 32 g |
| 80 kg (example profile) | 128–176 g | about 145 g | about 36 g |
| 90 kg | 144–198 g | about 160 g | about 40 g |

- **Creatine monohydrate**, 3–5 g a day: widely researched for strength and muscle. The evidence and cautions are in [Supplements](16-supplements.md).
- **Sleep 7–9 hours.** With early-morning training, that means a fixed bedtime.

## Measuring progress

| What | How | How often | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Body weight** | Morning **Check-in**, same conditions; the hub uses your 7-day average | Daily | Whether you're inside your band (one verdict a fortnight) |
| **Waist** | Tape halfway between your lowest rib and the top of your hip bone (the NHS method), end of a normal breath out. Same method every time | Every 2–4 weeks (Progress → Measurements) | Whether the gain is mostly lean (waist roughly stable) |
| **Thigh** | Mid-thigh, same marked spot | Every 2–4 weeks (Progress → Measurements) | Leg muscle coming back |
| **Chest and arms** | Each arm separately | Every 4 weeks | Upper-body regain, and your injured arm catching up |
| **Training log** | Loads and reps, plus estimated-1RM charts (for charts only, never tested) | Every session | Whether you're actually getting stronger; the leg-press target is +30% on your re-entry baseline by the end of Phase 1 |
| **Photos (optional)** | Same light, same time, same pose; front, side and back | Every 4–8 weeks | Changes the tape misses |

**Adjusting food is [Nutrition](14-nutrition.md)'s job.** That chapter owns the food rules, and the hub's weight verdicts follow the bands above. From the muscle side, two checks matter: your waist stays roughly steady while your weight rises, and your lifts and thighs keep going up. If both hold, change nothing **[Expert opinion]**.

> [!TIP]
> Don't judge the scale day to day. Salt, carbohydrate, hydration and a long walk can shift it by a kilo overnight. Judge the fortnightly verdict and the 4-weekly tape measurements together.

## The upper body: when and how

| Gate | What you can do for upper-body muscle |
|---|---|
| Now | Nothing loaded. Your arms rest. Your legs carry the growth. |
| Good arm OK | The seated good-arm block (Mon/Wed/Fri, Phases 0–1): one-arm chest press, row, curls, wrist curls and grip, 3 sets each at a moderate RIR 2–3. This builds your uninjured side and may help your injured arm keep strength. |
| Light use / rehab started | Your physio's programme only for your injured arm. Keep the good-arm block going. |
| Grip cleared | Two weeks of machines and cables only (Phase 2a), then light dumbbells (Phase 2), then real hypertrophy volume as the arm tolerates it (Phase 3), all at RIR 2–3. Expect chest, back and arms to come back quickly once they're loaded again. |
| Full clearance | Incline push-ups and hangs first (Phase 4a), then barbells, push-ups and pull-ups. Now upper-body volume can rise to 8–10+ sets per muscle, and RIR 1–2 is fine on machines. |

At every gate the **arm pain rule** applies: follow your physio's limit. If you weren't given one, keep pain at 2/10 or below during the exercise, back to your usual level by the next morning. Never train through pain that's increasing.

**About asymmetry.** After months of protection, your injured forearm and upper arm will be noticeably smaller and weaker than the other side. That's expected. Muscle comes back with training, though grip after a forearm fracture can take a long time to recover fully ([Farmer Strength](09-farmer-strength.md)). Don't add extra sets on the injured side to "even things up" unless your physio prescribes it. Train both sides with the same weight, set by your injured arm, and let time close the gap. Your grip readings on the **Your arm** page (taken by your physio, or with their OK) show the gap as a percentage on the grip ladder (70%, 80%, 90%). Track both arms' measurements on Progress → Measurements so you can see it happening.

## Common mistakes and fixes

- **Chasing your old numbers.** Fix: your pre-injury bests aren't this phase's targets. Your muscles may be ready before your tendons, and your arm sets the pace for anything it touches.
- **Programme-hopping.** Fix: keep the same main exercises for a whole phase; progress them.
- **"Junk" sets far from failure.** Fix: work sets end at RIR 1–3. If you finish a bodyweight set with 6 reps left, slow the lowering, add a pause or move to the harder version.
- **Grinding to failure for the pump.** Fix: stop at the target RIR, especially on single-leg work.
- **Under-eating on hike days.** Fix: eat more on Saturdays and Sundays, not just on gym days.
- **Panicking about the upper body.** Fix: it will come back, and quickly once it's cleared. Build the legs and engine now.
